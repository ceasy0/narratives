# Ruth — Beat Sheet

> The structure scene by scene: what each scene does, where Naomi stands in her arc, and how the episode is paced.

**Status:** v1, 2026-10-02, written alongside the first-draft script ([`scripts/ruth/ruth.fountain`](../../../scripts/ruth/ruth.fountain)), so the page numbers below are real, not estimates. It follows [treatment v4](./TREATMENT.md). **Checkpoint:** your pass on the script.

For the pilot I've folded what they'd say about story into the tables below (what each scene does, its passage, its additions) and left the production side (props, costumes, light) for after the script is locked, when it can be pulled straight from the final scenes instead of drafted twice. ## 1. The shape

**Length.** 37 pages, which is about 40 to 45 minutes on screen. A page of script runs about a minute, and pages like these, with a lot of silent action, run a little longer. The treatment estimated 58 minutes; the script is the better measure, and the animatic will be the real one (ROADMAP §15.4).

| Part | Pages | Share | Story time | Naomi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–4 | 10% | One morning | **Full** |
| Act One, in Moab | 4–14 | 26% | Twelve years | **Emptying** |
| Act One, the road and the return | 14–21 | 19% | About a week | **Empty** |
| Act Two | 21–28 | 20% | Seven weeks, told in two days | **Refilling** |
| Act Three | 28–32 | 11% | One night | **Refilling, and afraid** |
| Act Four | 32–37 | 14% | One morning, then a year | **Full** |

**The episode is a V,** and Naomi's arc is its spine. It runs down from full to empty for the first 21 pages, and back up to full in the last 16. The bottom is the well and the ruin, a little past the middle (pages 20–21). The oath on the road, the story's great turn, sits almost exactly at the center (page 18).

Three echoes do most of the work, and none is pointed at:
- **The laugh:** private in the prologue, gone for most of the episode, and out loud at the naming.
- **The dance:** at her sons' weddings in Moab, and at Ruth's in Bethlehem.
- **The lullaby:** hummed over her dying son, sung over her grandson.

## 3. Pacing

**Your concern** was that a long Act One could make the episode feel slow at the start and rushed at the end. Here's how the draft handles it, and what to watch in the animatic.

**Story time slows down while screen time holds steady.** The first ten pages cover twelve years, in short scenes with hard cuts. The road home covers a week in seven pages. Act Two covers one day, then a cut, then one more day. Act Three is one night in real time. So the episode doesn't run slow, then fast. It starts fast through the years and settles into hours. That's the shape of a story closing in on its climax.

**The slowdown starts inside Act One,** so there's no change of gear at the act break. The parting at the edge of the plateau is the longest single scene in the episode (five pages, in real time). Everything after it, the river, the pillar, the well, the ruin, is already moving at the pace Act Two will keep. Act One ends on a single evening image, and Act Two begins the next morning.

**The one fast stretch at the end is the book's own.** After the gate, a wedding, a pregnancy and a birth go by in three pages. The text does the same: 4:13 puts marriage, conception and birth into one verse. It mirrors the fast years in Moab at the start, so the episode is fast at both ends and slow in the middle, like a frame. What keeps it from feeling rushed is that each of the three gets one clear image (the wing, the hand on the belly, the birth), and the birth and the naming get room to breathe.

**Act Three looks short on the page** (four pages), because most of it is silent: the washing, the waiting below the floor, the near discovery, the night lying awake. It will play longer than it reads.

**Proportions.** The first half, the prologue and Act One, is 21 of 37 pages, about 57%. Your notes opened up Moab on purpose, and the invention sits there (§5). If the animatic shows Moab dragging, these are the first cuts I'd suggest, in order:
1. The plowing beat could fold into the grave scene: Mahlon's "You're sweating" plays over the burial instead.
2. The supper at Ruth's house could lose its first two lines.
3. The market could start on "There's bread in Bethlehem."

If the end feels rushed instead, we add time, not words: a longer hold on the birth, and on the roof.

**Transitions.** Every scene hands the next one something: a question, an object, a sound or a cut on action. The act breaks are fades because the treatment calls for them, and each lands on an image, never a line: rain over Moab, barley in evening light, Naomi's eyes on the oil flask, "Wait," and Obed's face.

## 5. Where the invention sits

From the script's own count (the tier mix at its end): 214 speeches, 48% recorded (Tier B), 1% implied (C), 51% invented (D).

| Part | Speeches | Recorded | Invented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 20 | 0 | 20 |

The invention is where your notes asked for it: the families, and the Moab years, where the text has no dialogue at all. From the moment Naomi turns to face her daughters-in-law on the road, the episode is mostly the book's own words, and the threshing floor is all of them.

## 6. What the animatic should test

1. **Moab's pace.** Do the ten pages in Moab feel like a story or a summary? Each scene should hand the next a question.
2. **The edge.** Five pages in real time. Does it hold?
3. **Naomi's markers.** Can someone who doesn't know the book point to where she's full, where she's empty and where she fills again?
4. **Act Three's length.** Does the silent night play long enough?
5. **The coda.** Do the wedding, the hand and the birth feel like a release rather than a rush?
6. **Names.** Does the audience know Ruth's name before Act Three? (Her father says it on page 5, and Orpah on page 17.)
