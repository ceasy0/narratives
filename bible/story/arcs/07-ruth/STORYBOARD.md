## 1. Two rules the boards follow

Both are mine (P6), for you to keep or strike.

1. **Toward the camera is home.** The series' camera faces east (the Opening's band walks north, which is screen left, with the view facing Eden's "east"). So in Ruth the road to Moab runs *away* from us, down into the rift and across it, and the return comes *toward* us. The view east (P7) is over Elimelech's shoulder; the parting on the road (1.21) has Orpah going back right and up, and Naomi and Ruth coming down-left toward the Jordan and the camera. If you'd rather Ruth keep its own compass, say so; the only cost is one line in each panel.
2. **Naomi's state is in the frame's fullness.** Full: the frame is crowded and warm (the bread bin, the wedding, the naming). Empty: the frame is wide, flat and nearly bare (the floor at dawn, the grave nobody comes to, the well). The V of the beat sheet (BEATS §1) is drawn as how much is in the frame.

## 3. What a full storyboard would add

When Ruth comes back off the shelf and the script is locked: a board for every scene (about seventy), the cuts inside each (the parting alone is a dozen), and the props and costumes from the dossier's production notes. That's the stage after your pass on the script (the pilot plan, step 5), and it isn't worth doing while scenes can still move.

## 4. A note from drawing them

Two things the boards showed that the script doesn't say out loud.

- **The three threshing floors are one set.** The bare floor at dawn (P1), Moab's floor (1.1) and the floor at midnight (3.5) are the same shape in the frame: an oval, centre, with people at its edge. Empty, foreign, then full and dark. Worth building once and lighting three ways.
- **Ruth is small until the field.** In every frame before 2.5 she is at the edge or in the background, watching. Boaz is the first person to bring her to the centre of a frame. If the camera keeps to that, the oath on the road (1.21) is the one exception, and it should feel like one.
