# The Opening — Dossier

> Genesis 1:1–2:7 as one fast sequence, from static to a human face above the Nile delta. What the text says, how your reading maps onto it, where it fits and where it pulls, the science, and what the prototype shows.

**Status:** v4.2, 2026-10-15: the research behind the [treatment](./TREATMENT.md), and every decision on the Opening in its current form. How it gets built is the [production plan](../../../../production/opening/PLAN.md). Built from your *Scene Descriptions* draft, [FR-01](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-01--the-opening-creation-as-cosmic-and-biological-history), [FR-02](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-02--god-yin-and-yang-as-the-trinity), [FR-15](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-15--genesis-1-read-in-order-as-the-history-of-matter), [FR-16](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-16--the-faces), [Discussion 001](../../../../discussions/001-how-god-speaks.md) and the prototype in `Biblical Story Concept`.

**Your brief:** it starts with a face emerging from a sea of black and white static and ends on the face of an early human looking at the Nile delta. It runs 5:07, ten seconds of it a fade from black. It should feel very fast, following evolution from a single-celled organism to humans just coming out of Africa. It's the introduction to Episode 1, which runs about an hour and continues through the Garden of Eden to Genesis 3:24.

## 1. The scope

The sequence runs from 1:1 to **2:7**. The last three passages each give it something:

| Verses | What they say | What it gives the sequence |
|---|---|---|
| 2:1–3 | God finishes and rests on the seventh day | A place to stop: the cliff, in real time |
| 2:5–6 | No cultivated plants yet, "for the LORD God had not caused it to rain on the land, and there was no man to work the ground," and a "mist" or "stream" (*'ed*) rose and watered "the whole face of the ground" | A land watered by a rising river and not by rain, before anyone farms |
| 2:7 | God forms the man from "dust from the ground" and breathes into his nostrils the breath of life | The whole sequence is the forming. The breath is the last thing in it. |

**On 1:1.** There's more behind your reading than you may know.

- **For it.** "The heavens and the earth" is a merism: Hebrew names a whole by its two ends, and it has no single word for "universe." So the Bible's first sentence does name everything as a pair. Reading 1:1 as a heading over the chapter is a standard view. And reading "heaven" as the spiritual creation and "earth" as the material one is Augustine's reading (*Confessions* XII), and Philo's before him. The Nicene Creed glosses the verse the same way: "maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible."
- **Against it.** A merism means "everything." It doesn't mean "twoness as such." And the second half of your sentence, that fundamental duality in turn creates God, pulls hard against several texts. That belongs to [Discussion 007](../../../../discussions/007-the-faces-of-god.md).

**On 1:6–8.** Three things to know.

- **The text names the vault.** "God called the vault Sky" (1:8). Its first hearers pictured something firm with water above it (Job 37:18, "hard as a cast metal mirror"; Gen 7:11, "the windows of the heavens"). Your reading takes the word as reaching for something its writer had no name for, the same move you make with "heavens and earth."
- **Who makes it.** 1:7 says "God made the vault and separated the waters." Your "created through the interaction between both separated waters" has to mean that this is how God made it.
- **The physics.** Physics counts four fundamental forces, not two, so "two fundamental forces" is your claim (P5) and not a finding. But physics does have a twoness at the bottom: every kind of particle has an opposite, and in the first instants the universe was a sea of such pairs appearing and vanishing. That's what beat 5 shows. "Space is nothing but the relations between things" is a serious position, held by Leibniz against Newton and by some physicists now. It isn't settled.

**The flying things of 1:20.** On your reading the days are in order, so "let flying things fly above the earth across the face of the vault" comes before the land animals of 1:24. The Hebrew word, *'oph*, covers every flying creature, insects included (Lev 11:20–23). The swimmer sees wings from underwater before it hauls out, and the air over the shore is alive with insects. The science allows it loosely: one genetic estimate puts flying insects around 400 million years ago, before any backboned animal walked, though the oldest winged fossils are later, about 325 million (low-to-medium confidence). After that, wings are never absent: pterosaurs and birds over the dinosaurs' world, and birds over the delta.

### On 2:4–7

**Your reading:** "I truly think it's possible that Genesis 2:4–7 is really just a recap or summary of Genesis 1:1–2:3 rather than a line of events afterwards."

**I think you're mostly right, and the Opening is already built that way.** It ends at 2:7, and it shows 2:4–7 inside the seventh day, at the cliff, not as something that happens after it. Here's the case both ways.

For it:

- **Genesis does the same thing again, in nearly the same words.** The next section opens: "This is the book of the generations of Adam. When God created man, he made him in the likeness of God. Male and female he created them, and he blessed them" (5:1–2). That's a recap of 1:26–28 at the head of a new section, and its "when" is the same Hebrew as 2:4's "in the day" (*beyom*). Genesis keeps doing it: a new section begins by going back over the end of the last one (6:9–10 after 5:32; 11:10 after 10:21–31; 25:19 after 25:1–11). 2:4–7 is the first time.
- **"In the day."** 2:4 says the LORD God made the earth and the heavens "in the day," a single day, where chapter 1 took seven. That's the language of a summary.
- **2:5–6 is background, not events.** In the Hebrew it's a string of "when" clauses: when no shrub of the field was yet in the land, and no plant of the field had yet sprouted, because there was no rain and no one to work the ground, and a mist kept rising... Nothing actually happens until 2:7. It has the same shape as 1:1–2.
- **The plants of 2:5 don't contradict 1:11–12.** "Shrub of the field" and "plant of the field" are what needs rain and what needs a farmer: the growth of open country, and grain ("you shall eat the plants of the field," 3:18). Mark Futato's study of these verses ("Because It Had Rained," *Westminster Theological Journal*, 1998) argues that 2:4–25 is arranged by topic, not by time, and Umberto Cassuto read 2:5 the same way before him. What's missing in 2:5 is what comes with people.
- **"A living creature."** The *nephesh chayyah* of 2:7 is the phrase 1:20–24 uses for the animals, and "the breath of life" belongs to all flesh in 7:22. So 2:7 describes what every human being is: dust, and breath, breathed into by God. Psalm 103:14 says it of everyone: "he remembers that we are dust."

Against it:

- **"These are the generations of" looks forward.** Every other time Genesis uses the formula (5:1; 6:9; 10:1; 11:10; 11:27; 25:12; 25:19; 36:1; 36:9; 37:2), the section that follows is about what came *from* the one named. "The generations of Terah" is mostly Abraham's story, and "the generations of Jacob" is Joseph's. So "the generations of the heavens and the earth" introduces what came out of them: a sequel. My answer: 5:1 shows the formula can open with a recap and then move on.
- **2:7 runs straight into 2:8.** "Then the LORD God formed the man... And the LORD God planted a garden in Eden, in the east, and there he put the man whom he had formed." It's one chain of verbs. If 2:7 is purely a recap, then the man God puts in the garden is the humankind of 1:27, one man standing for all of it.
- **The order still differs later.** In 2:19 God forms the animals "out of the ground" and brings them to the man, after he's made; in 1:24–27 they come first. Some translations (NIV, ESV) read it as "had formed" to bring the two into line. That's past your recap, in the Garden, so it's a question for [Discussion 008](../../../../discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md).
- **Many scholars read 2:4b–25 as a second creation story from a different source,** with its own order, and so neither a recap nor a sequel.

**Where I land (mine).** 2:4–6 is a recap. 2:7 is the hinge. It recaps 1:27, the making of humankind, told from the ground up, and it's also the first event of the new story, because 2:8 picks up "the man whom he had formed." That's what the breath across the cut already does: drawn in at the cliff, among the humans of 1:27, and let out by Adam in the Garden. **Grade: Supported for 2:4–6 as a recap; Compatible for 2:7 as both recap and hinge.** Logged as [FR-18](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-18--genesis-247-as-a-recap).

**What it changes.** Nothing in the Opening. In the Garden it points one way on [Discussion 008](../../../../discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md)'s first question. If 2:7 describes everyone, then Adam isn't new in body. What's new is what happens to him from 2:8 on: he's taken, placed, given work and spoken to.

**What reading it in order costs.** Tying each day to a stage of physical history is called concordism, and it has three standing objections:

1. The chapter defines its own words: Day, Night, Sky, Earth, Seas (1:5, 8, 10).
2. Science moves, and a reading pinned to it moves too.
3. Your reading is symbolic for days one to three and literal for days five and six.

My answer to the third is your own design. The surround starts abstract and settles until the world makes ordinary sense. The words do the same: they start as placeholders and end as plain names. So the shift isn't an inconsistency; it's the come-down. **My grade for the whole reading (FR-15): Compatible as something shown and never narrated. Tension if it's asserted as what the writer meant to say.** The series only needs the first.

## 3. What fits

- **Genesis gives a face to everything.** *Penei*, "face of," appears five times between 1:2 and 2:6: the face of the deep and of the waters (1:2), the face of the vault (1:20), the face of all the earth (1:29), and the whole face of the ground (2:6). Then God breathes into a human one (2:7). Your rule that every beat has a face, "each a kind of face," is the Hebrew's own habit.
- **Light in a face.** "God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Cor 4:6). Paul joins Genesis 1:3 to a face. Your beat 2, the face turning white with its eyes opening, is that verse.
- **The two experiences are the face shown and the face hidden.** "The LORD make his face to shine upon you... and give you peace" (Num 6:25–26). "When you hide your face, they are dismayed; when you take away their breath, they die and return to their dust. When you send forth your Spirit, they are created, and you renew the face of the ground" (Ps 104:29–30). The word for "dismayed" there means terrified. That's a creation psalm, and it has your connection and separation, the breath, the dust and the face in two verses.
- **Both interactions are God's.** "I kill and I make alive; I wound and I heal" (Deut 32:39; also Job 5:18; Hos 6:1; 1 Sam 2:6). The large face that hurts and the large face that heals have a text.
- **A mirror, then a face.** "Now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face" (1 Cor 13:12), and the Bible's last chapter says "they will see his face" (Rev 22:4). A series that opens on a face in static has its ending waiting for it.
- **Separation is the chapter's own verb** (*badal*, five times). Your separation-and-connection pair is its structure.
- **John 1:5.** "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." The blow lands, but the small face is never destroyed: every wound is followed by a healing.
- **"Evening and morning."** The refrain that closes each day is dark and light taking turns. Your alternation is that refrain as a picture.
- **Dust.** *Adam* is formed from the *adamah*, the ground (2:7), and "you are dust" (3:19). Matter forged in a star, a body going into the soil, a tree out of the soil, and then people.
- **Rest.** The sequence never stops moving for four and a half minutes. The cliff is the first thing that plays in real time, and the stillness itself is the seventh day.
- **The breath.** *Ruach* means wind, breath and spirit. The static's hiss is wind over water (1:2), and the last sound is one human breath (2:7).
- **The face the ego sees.** "You thought that I was one like yourself" (Ps 50:21). "With the crooked you make yourself seem tortuous" (Ps 18:26). Your note that a separated part with an ego perceives God as malicious has these two verses behind it. And the laugh has texts: "He who sits in the heavens laughs" (Ps 2:4), and Wisdom's "I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when terror strikes you" (Prov 1:26), said to people who refused her.
- **Connection ends in sleep.** "In peace I will both lie down and sleep" (Ps 4:8); "he gives to his beloved sleep" (Ps 127:2). Sleep comes back at the Garden's turning point: the "deep sleep" in which the woman is made (2:21).
- **A child facing east.** The oldest burial in the Nile valley, at Taramsa Hill, is a child of eight or ten, laid seated in a shallow pit about 55,000 years ago, facing east with the face turned up to the sky (§5). That's the cliff's date and the band's river.
- **"In the east."** 2:8 says God planted a garden "in Eden, in the east," and put the man there. From the Nile, east is toward the Tigris and Euphrates (2:14). With the person at the cliff now an ancestor of Adam, the journey east is made by the generations in between ([Discussion 008](../../../../discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md)).

## 4. What pulls against it

**1. Death before Eden. Fit grade: Tension, adopted on purpose.**

You answered: "So it has to be read as human death before the Garden, and not spiritual death? That sounds right to me." The sequence shows animals dying for hundreds of millions of years and people dying for tens of thousands, all before Adam. For that to stand beside Rom 5:12 and 1 Cor 15:21, the death that comes through Adam can't be the first death of a body.

**The reading we're adopting: bodies died before the Garden, human ones included. What comes through Adam is spiritual death: being cut off from God and from the tree of life.** 
For it:

- "In the day that you eat of it you shall surely die" (Gen 2:17), and Adam lives 930 years (5:5). Whatever died that day, it wasn't his body.
- He's barred from the tree of life "lest he... live forever" (3:22–24). So he was mortal by nature, "you are dust" (3:19), and unending life was something offered and then lost.
- Rom 5:12 says death spread "to all men." Paul isn't talking about animals. And Eph 2:1 calls living people "dead in your trespasses."
- Psalm 104:21 praises God for giving lions their prey, inside a creation psalm.

Against it:

- **1 Cor 15:21–22 is about the resurrection of the body,** so the death it answers is the body's: "as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead." This is the strongest text against a purely spiritual reading. It holds this way: through Adam, death became humanity's fixed end, because the tree of life was lost. The verse doesn't say no body had died before. John Walton argues this in *The Lost World of Adam and Eve*.
- God calls the finished creation "very good" (Gen 1:31), and 1:29–30 gives every creature plants for food.

**Your note:** "I think the resurrection of Christ was also spiritual. Because Christ is a bloodline, he was resurrected through his children. Let me know if this removes the problem."

*My answer:* it turns on the word "also," and either way the problem is already handled.

- **If "also" means both,** the body raised *and* more than the body: **Supported.** Paul calls the raised body "a spiritual body" (1 Cor 15:44), and Christ was "made alive in the spirit" (1 Pet 3:18). Living on through children has texts of its own. Of the servant who dies: "he shall see his offspring; he shall prolong his days" (Isa 53:10). "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit" (John 12:24). "Behold, I and the children God has given me" (Heb 2:13). In those texts the children are his people; whether they're also his descendants is [FR-10](../../../../canon/FRAMINGS.md#fr-10--christ-as-a-bloodline-the-rainbow-widening-comings). But on this version 1 Cor 15:21 still answers a body's death with a body's rising, so it doesn't remove the problem.
- **If it means instead of the body: Contradiction.** It would remove the problem, but at a far higher cost. "See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch me, and see. For a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have" (Luke 24:39), and he eats fish in front of them (24:42–43). The tomb is empty (Mark 16:6). "Put your finger here" (John 20:27). "His flesh did not see corruption" (Acts 2:31). "Raised on the third day" (1 Cor 15:4) is three days, not a generation. And Christ is "the firstfruits; then at his coming those who belong to Christ" (1 Cor 15:23): he's raised first, and his people after.
- **It doesn't need removing.** The reading we adopted already carries 1 Cor 15:21. Bodies were mortal by nature ("you are dust," 3:19). Unending life was offered in the tree of life and lost through Adam (3:22–24). Christ's rising gives it back, which is why the tree of life returns at the end (Rev 22:2). A bodily resurrection fits that reading. Your bloodline idea can stand beside it without replacing it.

Logged under FR-10, for the Gospels.

It suits your framework better than I'd seen. The death Adam brings is separation, and separation is your word for the first thing that happens, in the first minute. That raises a question for the Garden: if separation is already there in beat 2, what does the Fall add? ([Discussion 008](../../../../discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md), question 5.)

**2. The face is God's, and all four faces are God.** You answered: "The malice is not in God. When one is separated and has an ego, they perceive a part of God as malicious." The dark half and the light half aren't the Father and the Son; they're the fundamental experiences. The detail is in [Discussion 007](../../../../discussions/007-the-faces-of-god.md). In short:

- Both interactions as God's acts: **Supported** (Deut 32:39).
- The face as God's: **Supported if the face is God's image,** which Gen 1:26 says is human and Col 1:15 says is Christ. **Tension** with Deut 4:15–16 and John 1:18 otherwise.
- Terror in God: it has one home in the texts, the Son in Gethsemane and on the cross (Mark 14:33–34; 15:34).
- Malice in God: **Contradiction as stated** (1 John 1:5; James 1:13; Lam 3:33), and **not what you mean.** The malice is how the whole looks to a separated part with an ego (Ps 18:26; 50:21). **Supported in that form.**

All four faces are one face, and the malice appears only after the cut. The dark face climbs to a mad, laughing ego, and since it's the small face's own face, enlarged, the picture says your reading by itself.

**3. Reading the days in order.** See §2: Compatible as shown, Tension as a claim about the writer's meaning.

**4. No words.** Genesis 1 is mostly speech: ten times "God said," and in 1:28–30 God speaks to the people he's made. You've decided that the speech stands for what God did or thought, and that nothing is spoken. The recorded words are the default (principle 4), so this is logged as a decision in [FOUNDATIONS](../../../../canon/FOUNDATIONS.md#4-decisions-so-far). Ps 33:6, 9 and Heb 11:3 read the chapter's speech the same way.

**5. A first man with ancestors.** Paul calls Adam "the first man" (1 Cor 15:45, 47). If the person at the cliff is Adam's ancestor, "first" can't be a head-count. It needn't be: the same verse calls Christ "the second man," and he wasn't the second human being. Graded Tension, and taken up in [Discussion 008](../../../../discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md).

## 5. The science

**The two dates you asked me to check.**

| Question | What I found | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **When did the delta form?** | The delta we know began building about 7,500 years ago, when the rise of the sea after the ice age slowed down. Before that the same ground was an older river plain.  | High |
| **When did humans leave Africa?** | Everyone alive outside Africa descends from a movement about 50,000–60,000 years ago. Two lines of evidence: the genetic split between Egyptians and non-Africans is put at about 55,000 years, and the mixing with Neanderthals that all non-Africans carry is dated to about 50,000–43,000 years ago, so they'd left by then. Earlier groups did leave (fossils in Israel at about 180,000 and 100,000 years), but they left few or no descendants. | Medium-high on the range |
| **Which way out?** | Two routes are argued: down the Nile and across Sinai, or over the mouth of the Red Sea into Arabia. The genomes of living Egyptians and Ethiopians favor the Nile. | Medium |

**Decided: the date humans left.** The cliff is set **about 55,000 years ago.** The gap from there to Adam and Eve is long, which you've said is fine.

**What the delta looked like then.** A wide, sandy plain crossed by braided channels that ran with the seasons, partly green. The sea stood tens of metres lower, so the coast lay well beyond today's. The valley still opened where it opens now, at today's Cairo, so the Mokattam escarpment still works as the cliff, and "opens like a hand into many channels" is still true. It was paler and less lush than the delta we know. (Medium confidence.)

**Greener than that, at your request.** You asked for the Nile to be a bit more lush, and the evidence leaves room. A sea-floor pollen record off the delta shows more pollen washed down from the Nile between about 56,000 and 44,000 years ago, when a stronger summer monsoon over Ethiopia made the river run higher. In Arabia, river channels came back to life around 55,000 years ago. Several researchers tie the dispersal out of Africa to exactly these wetter spells. So the river runs high, with reeds, papyrus, trees along the banks and hippos, and the delta green along every channel and between them, with mist and birds. It's still a braided plain and not today's fan. **License, with support:** the wetter spell is medium confidence; how green it was at the cliff on that morning is our choice.

### The route (beat 13)

Your note (2026-10-15): the band walks the whole way from middle or southern Africa to the Nile, so the country should change along the way, and be accurate for about 55,000 years ago. Nobody can name the road one band took. What the evidence gives is where the people who left most likely came from, a corridor, the country along it, and how it differed from today.

**Where the walk starts.**

| Question | What I found | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Where did the people who left live just before? | Most likely eastern Africa. The mitochondrial line every non-African carries, L3, arose there about 70,000 years ago, and it's the same age as its first daughter lines outside Africa, so one expansion probably made both. | Medium |
| Middle Africa? | Plausible. The forest was never a wall: people were living in West Africa's rainforest by about 150,000 years ago (Bété I, in Côte d'Ivoire). In the Semliki valley, where the Congo forest meets the western Rift, people made barbed bone points for spearing catfish about 90,000 years ago (Katanda). The Semliki is a headwater of the Nile: it runs north into Lake Albert, and the Nile runs out of Lake Albert. The valley is the border of Congo and Uganda, so a start there is middle Africa and eastern Africa at once. | High (Bété I); medium (Katanda's date) |
| Southern Africa? | People were there (the southern Cape's caves hold some of the richest Middle Stone Age record), but it's the least likely start for the band at the cliff: the southern lines branched off from the rest more than 260,000 years ago, and the line that left Africa is closest to eastern Africans. One band can come from anywhere, so it isn't impossible. | Low |

**How the land differed from today.**

| | About 55,000 years ago | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| The world | Early in the middle stretch of the last ice age: cooler than now, the sea tens of metres lower, and carbon dioxide at about 200–220 parts per million (280 before industry). That little carbon dioxide favors grass over trees: in southeast Africa, open country spread whenever it fell that low. | Medium |
| The Nile basin | Mostly dry from 75,000 to 15,000 years ago, with dunes encroaching on the river, but with a moist phase at about 55,000–50,000. The Nile then ran very high: the dark layer it left on the eastern Mediterranean floor (sapropel S2) is dated about 55,000, and the Blue Nile's floods spread sand and clay over the plain south of where Khartoum is now. | Medium |
| Eastern Africa and the Sahara | A wet phase from about 59,000 to 48,000 years ago in eastern Africa and Arabia: Lake Tana high at about 54,000, grass spreading in the Sahara between 61,000 and 40,000 (most at about 51,000), and strong Nile flow from 60,000 to 50,000. **The case against:** a core from the Gulf of Aden reads the Horn of Africa as cold and dry when people left (about 65,000–55,000), and its authors think the drying pushed them out. | Medium |
| Middle Africa | Wetter only later: tropical Africa has almost no dated wet records before about 53,000 years ago. So the Congo forest was probably smaller and more broken than now, with more open woodland and grass along its edges. | Low to medium |
| The high mountains | Colder, with far more ice. Ethiopia's Simien has moraines dated about 68,000 and 46,000 years ago. The Rwenzori's oldest dated moraines are later (about 23,000–20,000 years, reaching down to 2,000 m), but at 55,000 its ice would have reached well below today's last scraps. | Medium |
| The great lakes | Little known. One Lake Victoria record reads drier from about 100,000 to 35,000 years ago, which would have thinned the White Nile's steady flow, but it's unresolved. | Low |

**Two routes.**

- **A. From the Semliki valley, down the Nile's own course (my pick).** It starts at the Congo forest's eastern edge in the Semliki valley, under the Rwenzori, the range long identified with Ptolemy's "Mountains of the Moon," where the Nile was said to rise. It goes north down the valley past Lake Albert, across the woodland of South Sudan to its flat grass plains, north through the Sahel to where the Blue and White Niles meet, and down the Nile through Nubia and Egypt to the delta: about 3,200 km as the crow flies, with the river reached a little past halfway. Why it's mine: it's middle Africa, where the genetics points to eastern Africa; going north, the country runs forest, woodland, grassland, dry country, river, which is the order the layout already has, so the layout keeps its shape and changes only its dressing and its distances; and the walk is the length of the Nile, from a headwater to the delta.
- **B. From southern Africa, up the east.** Lake Malawi's woodland (the lake was high, and the country close to today's, after about 60,000 years ago), the Serengeti's plains under Kilimanjaro's ice, Lake Victoria's shore, then route A from South Sudan on: about 4,700 km from Lake Malawi as the crow flies, 7,000 from the Cape. The landmarks are grander, but the start is less likely (above), the forest at the start would be a strip of riverside forest in woodland country, and there's more country than shots.

**Where the land can jump.** Generations pass where a trunk crosses the lens: at 04:15:19, 04:22:12, 04:26:07 and 04:33 ([PLAN §9](../../../../production/opening/PLAN.md#9-what-i-need-from-you)). The first two fall inside one chase: the cat is after the band across the first, and the man who kills it at 04:19 is the one who falls at the clash after the second. So the land can't jump at either of them, and strictly no generation can pass there either ([README](../../../../README.md#what-i-need-from-you), item 1). The long jumps go at the last two: the end of the clash, and the lone tree after the newborn. Every change of country between jumps is one a walker would see in a day: a forest edge, woodland thinning onto a floodplain, grass giving way to sand above a river.
