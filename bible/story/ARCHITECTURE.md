# Architecture — the whole Bible as episodes

> The series on one page, as [REVIEW §2.1](../REVIEW.md#21-write-the-whole-bible-map-before-the-gardens-treatment) proposed and you chose (2026-10-08, "Yes, map first"). Every episode in one line: its text, when it happens, its heart, and what it waits on. It's a map, not a treatment: nothing here is written until its arc comes, and every line can move.

**Status:** v1.1, 2026-10-08 (evening). Applies your names and your count for Arc 1 (Genesis, eight hours: Noah in three, the nations in three), your name for Season 2, and renumbers every hour after it by two. v1 built from your draft (`Bible_Structure.md`, seasons 1–3 and the book outline to Judges), the roadmap's arc map (§10.3), the working chronology, and everything decided so far. **What's yours:** the shape of seasons 1–3 and most of their titles. **What's mine:** the density (§2), the merges in Genesis (§10), the placements where the text isn't in order (§3), everything from Genesis 30 on, and the threads (§8). Episode counts are the honest number for about an hour each, not a target.

---

## 1. How to read it

- **An arc** is a self-contained story, numbered as the roadmap numbers them (Ruth's folder is `07-ruth` because Judges and Ruth are Arc 7). **A season** is a production run of six to twelve episodes; big arcs split across seasons (§9).
- **Text** is what the hour dramatizes. Passages placed inside it from elsewhere (a psalm, a prophet's oracle, a law) are in §4–§6.
- **When** follows [`world/TIMELINE.md`](../world/TIMELINE.md). Dates before Abraham are undated or provisional.
- **Heart** is the one event the hour is built to land, in a few words, with the set-ups it carries for later hours where that matters (your Sodom principle, §2).
- **Waits on** names the discussion, Position or framing the hour can't be treated without. Blank means nothing beyond what's decided.
- **Could split / could merge** is marked where I'm unsure of the length.

## 2. The rules the map runs on

1. **About an hour, and the text's own moment is the climax.** An episode holds one event the hour builds to. Where the text is dense (Genesis 24, 2 Samuel 18) one chapter is an hour; where it's thin (genealogies, lists of kings) several chapters are one hour; where it isn't story (law, psalms, oracles, letters) it's placed inside an hour that is (§4–§6).
2. **The why comes earlier.** Your principle: a scene in an earlier episode gives the context for a later one, so that when Sodom burns the audience already knows Sodom. The map marks these as "sets up." It's how the gaps get filled without lectures, and it's the same thing the Opening does for the Garden.
3. **Chronological order, including the five places the Bible's own order isn't** (§3). You decided the structure can be anything that can be put in chronological order; the map simply puts it there.
4. **Density: about one and a half to two chapters of narrative an hour**, which is Ruth's density with your expansions. Your draft runs at about one chapter an hour through Abraham. At that density the whole Bible is roughly 350 hours; at this one, about 200. I've used this one and listed every merge (§10) so you can restore any of them. My reason: at one chapter an hour, most of each hour is Tier D and P6, and the REVIEW's warning about the Garden would apply to the whole of Genesis. The hours still have room for the context you want; they have less room for invented talk.
5. **Kings is the spine; Chronicles adds.** Chronicles tells the same story for the returned exiles. Where it adds a scene that Kings doesn't have (Jehoshaphat's singers, Manasseh's chains, Zechariah stoned in the court), the scene goes in. Where the two differ, Kings leads and the difference is noted (Position 2).
6. **A prophet is a character inside the history** (ROADMAP §10.4). Each prophetic book is placed in the hours its prophet lived through (§5). Where the book's own date is uncertain, the default is named and flagged.
7. **Law is lived, not read.** Each law book keeps the stories it contains (Nadab and Abihu, the blasphemer, the wood-gatherer, Zelophehad's daughters), and its rules become customs the characters keep on screen without comment. The one exception is Deuteronomy, which is a speech and gets staged as one: an old man's farewell.
