# The Opening — how the beginning has been pictured

> Every notable depiction of Genesis 1:1–2:7 I could find and check, across every medium and period, from the burned Cotton Genesis to VR: the creation of the heavens and the earth, the six days, the seventh, and the man formed from the dust. You asked for "every visual depiction ever made" of the beginning (2026-10-15). That can't literally be listed, so this is the fullest catalogue I could build, with its scope and what's left out said plainly below. It serves the Opening ([treatment](../../story/arcs/01-beginnings/opening/TREATMENT.md)): the [look book](./REFERENCES.md) keeps what each shot takes, and the films worth watching from here are on the [watch list](../../STUDY.md).

**Status:** 2026-10-15: 122 entries in thirteen sections, 107 of them pictures of Gen 1:1–2:7 and 15 neighbours, with my ten for the Opening at the top; † marks a detail I couldn't confirm.

## How it's scoped

- **In:** pictures of 1:1–2:3 (the beginning, the deep, the seven days) and 2:4–7 (the man formed from the dust and given breath), in any medium: manuscripts, mosaics, glass, sculpture, painting, prints, photography, comics, stage, film, animation, games and VR.
- **Notable** means at least one of these: it set or changed how the beginning is pictured; it's a major monument of its tradition; it reached a very large audience; or it bears on a choice we're making in the Opening.
- **Out:** Eden from 2:8 on (the garden, Eve, the Fall), except where a cycle runs straight on from 2:7, in which case the entry says so; the routine examples (every Book of Hours, every illuminated *In principio*, every children's Bible) beyond one or two that stand for the rest; music with no staged or filmed form (Haydn's *Creation*); and anything I couldn't find a reliable page for. What I looked for and couldn't find or confirm is listed in the last section.
- **Neighbours** are marked *(neighbour)*: not Genesis, but close enough to the Opening to study, because they picture the beginning of the world or of life without the text.
- **Checks.** Every link was opened on 2026-10-15. The facts were checked against the linked page or a second source; † marks a detail I couldn't confirm. Some museum sites (the Met, MoMA, the National Gallery of Art, the Morgan, the British Museum) block scripted checks; their records were confirmed through search. The descriptions are mine.
- **Each entry:** the work; its maker or tradition; date; where it is; medium; the verses it shows; one reliable link; then a line on what's distinctive about how it pictures the beginning.

## Ten that matter most for the Opening

My picks, in order. Each is in its section below with its details.

1. **The Sarajevo Haggadah's creation cycle** (c. 1350). The Spirit is dark waves over white water, the first day is the frame divided into a black half and a white half, and the seventh day is a man sitting at rest. Its first day is our beats 2–3 in one frame, and it gives the seventh day a picture of its own, as we do. It's also the case against our face: it never draws God, which is the Jewish line (Deut 4:15–16) that the [dossier](../../story/arcs/01-beginnings/opening/DOSSIER.md) weighs against a face of God.
2. **The Tapestry of Creation, Girona** (c. 1100). A young man's face, Christ as Creator, at the centre of a wheel, with the works of creation around him in segments. It's the oldest version of our rule 3 (one steady face while everything turns around it), and it rests on the same ground as our first face: Christ as the image of the invisible God (Col 1:15; [Discussion 007](../../discussions/007-the-faces-of-god.md)).
3. **Paul Nash, *Genesis*** (1924). Twelve woodcuts that start from *The Void* and cut each day's forms out of the black. The nearest thing in print to our beats 1–11, abstract first and the world later, and a model for the graphic, vibrant register ([Discussion 006](../../discussions/006-2d-or-3d.md)).
4. **The Saint John's Bible, *Creation*** (2003). Seven strips: fractals and the Hebrew word for chaos, a gold line at "let there be light", a satellite photograph of a delta, rock-art people on the sixth day, the seventh all gold. It makes our move, the science of the world inside a sacred frame, and it even arrives where we do, at a delta (beat 14) and the first people (beat 13).
5. **Robert Fludd, *Utriusque Cosmi*** (1617). A square of black with "and so to infinity" on its four sides: the darkness before anything, drawn as a material with no edge, then the light breaking out of its centre plate by plate. Our frame 0 and the blast (beats 1–2).
6. **Ivan Aivazovsky, *Chaos*** (1841). A shape made only of light rising out of a storm over the sea. The nearest painting to beat 1, the face rising out of the deep, and a warning about how quickly that becomes a robed figure.
7. **Michelangelo, *The Separation of Light from Darkness*** (1512). The image the audience brings with them: God as a body twisting overhead, his face almost hidden, the separation done with his arms. Ours is all face and no body, done by a front of light (beats 2–3). Worth looking at closely so that we answer it rather than echo it by accident.
8. **Barnett Newman, *Day One* and *Genesis—The Break*** (1946–52). A field of colour divided by a single band: the first separation as the whole picture. The look of our front of light crossing the frame (beat 3), and proof that it can be the image on its own.
9. **Isaac Luria's *tzimtzum*, and Anselm Kiefer's *Zim Zum*** (16th c.; 1990). God draws in his infinite light to leave a space for the world; vessels of light then shatter into sparks. Your line in [Discussion 001](../../discussions/001-how-god-speaks.md), that God "deliberately puts a limit on himself", is this idea almost word for word, and the shattering is our beat 4. It's beyond the canon (P3), so it can be a visual cousin; using it as a source would need a discussion.
10. **Johann Jakob Scheuchzer, *Homo ex humo*** (1731). Adam framed by eleven human embryos and foetal skeletons: "man from the dust" told through the science of its day, growth and death in one plate. The precedent for beat 12, where the dust is a body returning to the ground and something growing from it.

Next after these: *Fantasia*'s *Rite of Spring* (the wordless, music-led creation every animator knows), Burne-Jones's *Days of Creation* (one angel per day holding the world in a globe), Čiurlionis's *Creation of the World* (a composer's thirteen panels) and Vinton and Gratz's *The Creation* (paint that moves like paint).

## What the tradition keeps doing

- **God shown, or not.** The Sarajevo Haggadah never draws him. The Cotton Genesis line (San Marco, Monreale, Girona) draws the Creator as the young Christ, the Word through whom all things were made (John 1:3). From the thirteenth century the West draws an old man, the Ancient of Days of Daniel 7:9. Our first face stands nearest the Christ line: a face, not a body.
- **The days as a set that grows:** circles filling up (the Nuremberg Chronicle), roundels down the stem of a letter (the Paris Bibles), one more angel each day (San Marco, Burne-Jones), strips side by side (the Saint John's Bible). Before the twentieth century almost nobody lets one day flow into the next. We do nothing else.
- **The vault as rings,** pushed or held by God: Giovanni di Paolo, Pisa, Padua, San Gimignano. That's the medieval picture of 1:6–8; Cosmas's box with a lid is the other one.
- **The compass** (the Vienna Bible moralisée, the Holkham Bible, Blake): creation as measuring. Blake turns it against itself.
- **Light as a body's act or as a split.** Michelangelo, Raphael, Doré and Wyspiański give the first day to a figure's arms; Sarajevo, Fludd and Newman give it to the picture itself.
- **2:7 as a hand on clay** (della Quercia, Rodin, Chagall), or, in Islamic painting, as the moment after, when the angels bow. Only Scheuchzer shows it as a process.
- **The seventh day is nearly always skipped.** San Marco, Sarajevo, the Nuremberg Chronicle, the Russian Sabbath icon, Barlach and the Saint John's Bible give it a picture; most cycles stop at Adam. Our 24 seconds at the cliff are rarer than they look.
- **Since 1900 the days have become science:** *Fantasia*, Haas, the Saint John's Bible, Malick, *Noah*. And since 1996 the screen keeps handing the telling to Noah on the ark (*Testament*, *The Bible*, *Noah*).

## 1. The first pictures (to 1100)

- **The Cotton Genesis** · Greek, probably made in Egypt · 4th–5th century · British Library, Cotton MS Otho B VI; eighteen charred fragments since the fire of 1731 · illuminated manuscript, about 340–360 miniatures · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Genesis)
  The source of the Western cycle. Its creation pictures are lost, but San Marco's dome copies them: a young, beardless Creator, and each day as a small winged figure, one more for each day.
- **The Tours Bibles: Moutier-Grandval, Vivian, Bamberg** · the scriptorium of Saint-Martin, Tours · c. 830–846 · British Library, Add MS 10546; BnF, lat. 1; Staatsbibliothek Bamberg, Msc. Bibl. 1 · illuminated manuscripts, full-page Genesis frontispieces · *2:7 and Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bible_of_Charles_the_Bald)
  Genesis as a page of four bands read like a strip, beginning with the Creator shaping Adam† and running on into Eden. The six days aren't there at all.
- **The Junius manuscript** · Anglo-Saxon · c. 1000 · Bodleian Library, MS Junius 11 · line drawings with the Old English verse *Genesis* · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junius_manuscript)
  The poem, and its drawings, start in heaven with the rebel angels' fall before the world is made†: creation as the answer to a rebellion, a framing our Opening doesn't use.
- **The Old English Hexateuch** · Canterbury, probably St Augustine's · second quarter of the 11th century · British Library, Cotton MS Claudius B IV · coloured drawings in the text · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_Hexateuch)
  The first illustrated Genesis in English; the Creator at work on each day in outline and wash†.
- **The Bernward Doors** · Bishop Bernward's workshop · 1015 · Hildesheim Cathedral · cast bronze, sixteen panels · *2:7 and Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernward_Doors)
  The top-left panel is read either as God making Adam or as Eve taken from Adam's side (the sources differ); the left door then falls panel by panel, away from God, down to Cain's murder.
- **The Tapestry of Creation** · unknown, Catalonia · late 11th or early 12th century · Museum of Girona Cathedral · wool embroidery on linen, 365 × 470 cm as it survives · *1:1–2:3, with Adam and Eve* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapestry_of_Creation)
  A young beardless Christ at the centre of a wheel, the works of creation in the segments round him, and the winds, months and seasons outside: one face held still while the world turns around it.
- **The Salerno ivories** · probably Amalfi or Salerno · late 11th–12th century · Museo Diocesano, Salerno · carved ivory plaques, between 38 and 70 of them · *1:2–25, then Eden onward* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno_Ivories)
  The first day is two discs, labelled *Lux* and *Nox*, and a plaque of its own for the sun, moon and stars, in a style that mixes Byzantine, Islamic and Romanesque.
- **Wiligelmo's Genesis reliefs** · Wiligelmo · c. 1099–1110 · west façade, Modena Cathedral · marble relief frieze · *2:7 and Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiligelmo)
  The first large Genesis in Romanesque sculpture: the Creator, Adam made, Eve taken from his side, in heavy figures pushing out of the stone.
- **The nave vault of Saint-Savin-sur-Gartempe** · unknown painters · c. 1095–1115 · Abbey Church of Saint-Savin, Vienne (UNESCO) · fresco on a barrel vault · *1:14–18, then Eden onward* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Church_of_Saint-Savin-sur-Gartempe)
  Christ makes the lights: the sun is a man's head in rays, the moon a woman's with a crescent. The lights given faces, nine centuries before our rule that every beat has one.

## 2. Byzantium, Russia and the Eastern churches

- **Cosmas Indicopleustes, the *Christian Topography*** · text c. 550; the oldest illustrated copy made in Constantinople in the 9th century · Vatican Library, Vat. gr. 699 (later copies at Sinai and Florence) · diagrams in a manuscript · *1:6–8* · [DigiVatLib](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Vat.gr.699)
  The vault taken at its word: the universe as a box with a curved lid, shaped like the Tabernacle, with the firmament across it and the waters above.
- **The Byzantine Octateuchs** · Constantinople · 11th–13th century · Vatican Library, Vat. gr. 746 and 747; Topkapı Palace, G.İ. 8 (the Seraglio Octateuch, made for Isaac Komnenos); Vatopedi, Mount Athos, 602; the Smyrna copy burned in 1922 · illuminated manuscripts · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraglio_Octateuch)
  The Eastern cycle beside the Cotton Genesis line, with a small framed scene in the text for each act of creation; I couldn't check how their Genesis 1 scenes are composed†.
- **The Genesis cycle at Visoki Dečani** · the Dečani painters · c. 1335–1350 · Visoki Dečani Monastery, Kosovo (UNESCO), the north chapel of St Demetrius · fresco in the domes · *1:1–2:7* · [BLAGO Fund](https://www.blagofund.org/Archives/Decani/Church/Pictures/Frescoes/Nave/North_Nave_Parecclesion_of_St_Demetrius/Third_Dome/)
  Said to be the only monumental creation cycle surviving in Eastern Christian painting; Dragana Van de Moortel (Oxford) reads it as a picture of time unfolding.
- **The Hexahemeron icon** · Russian icon painters · from the late 15th century · many collections · tempera on panel · *1:1–2:3* · [Russian Icon Collection](https://russianicon.com/the-monumental-orthodox-icons-of-the-hexahemeron/)
  Six creation scenes set beside the feasts of the six weekdays, so that the days of creation are also the days of the church's week.
- **"God Rested on the Seventh Day"** · a Russian icon type; also in the Four-Part Icon of the Kremlin's Annunciation Cathedral (c. 1547–51) · mid-16th century · Icon Museum and Study Center, Clinton, Massachusetts (R2013.80) · tempera on panel · *2:1–3* · [Icon Museum](https://www.iconmuseum.org/collection/god-rested-on-the-seventh-day-2/)
  The rarest subject here, the seventh day as an image of its own, and it was contested at once: in 1553–54 the clerk Ivan Viskovaty attacked the new icons that showed God the Father, and a church council defended them. How God is shown at rest I couldn't confirm†.
- **Dionysios of Fourna, the *Painter's Manual*** · Mount Athos · 1729–33 · text; standard edition by Papadopoulos-Kerameus, 1909 · *1:1–2:7* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysios_of_Fourna)
  A painter's instructions for every Old Testament scene, Genesis included: how the Orthodox world was taught to paint the days. I haven't read its passage on the first day†.
- **An Armenian Bible of 1623** · copied by Hakob for Khodja Nazar of New Julfa · Istanbul, 1623 · Calouste Gulbenkian Museum, Lisbon, LA152 · illuminated manuscript, a double-page opening to Genesis · *1:1–2:7 and Eden* · [Gulbenkian](https://gulbenkian.pt/museu/en/works/illuminated-bible/)
  The creation and Adam and Eve on one page; on the other, Christ Pantocrator, whose rays enclose the Holy Spirit and Moses, the writer of Genesis.

Aivazovsky, an Armenian Christian painting for an Armenian monastery, is in section 8.

## 3. Jewish art

- **Zodiac mosaics in ancient synagogues** *(neighbour)* · Hammat Tiberias (4th century), Sepphoris (5th–6th), Beth Alpha (6th) and others · in place, in Israel · floor mosaic · *1:14–18 at most* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zodiac_mosaics_in_ancient_synagogues)
  No ancient synagogue shows the days of creation, and Dura-Europos has no Genesis 1. The nearest is the zodiac wheel with the sun at its centre and the seasons in the corners: the lights "for signs and for seasons" under the congregation's feet.
- **The Sarajevo Haggadah, the creation cycle** · an unknown Jewish illuminator of the Crown of Aragon · c. 1350 (some say c. 1335), probably Barcelona · National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Sarajevo · illuminated manuscript, eight framed miniatures on two facing pages · *1:1–2:3* · [Visual Midrash](https://talivisualmidrash.org.il/en/creations/sarajevo-haggadah-the-seven-days-of-creation/)
  God isn't drawn. The Spirit is dark waves moving over white water; the first day is the frame divided into a black half and a white half; the world becomes a globe on the second; the seventh is a man seated at rest. The only Sephardi haggadah of its kind with a creation cycle.
- **The *tzimtzum* in Lurianic diagrams** · Isaac Luria's school in Safed and its copyists · 16th–18th century · manuscripts and printed editions of *Etz Chaim* and other works · diagrams · *1:1–3, as Kabbalah reads them* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzimtzum)
  Creation begins with God contracting his infinite light to leave an empty space, drawn as circles inside circles with one ray let back in†. Beyond the canon (P3); Kiefer's paintings of it are in section 9.
- **Shalom of Safed, *Genesis: The Separation of Light from Darkness*** · Shalom Moskovitz · 1960s (the museum's date is uncertain) · Philadelphia Museum of Art · painting, medium not given† · *1:3–5* · [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://www.philamuseum.org/objects/268522)
  A Safed watchmaker turned naïve painter, who told Genesis and Exodus in bright bands of scenes with the verses written in.
- **Archie Rand, *The Chapter Paintings*: "Bereshit"** · Archie Rand · 1989 · first shown at the Jewish Museum, New York · 54 large paintings, one for each weekly Torah portion · *1:1–6:8, the portion Bereshit* · [Jewish Art Salon](https://jewishartsalon.org/archie-rand/archie-rand-the-54-chapter-paintings/)
  The first of 54, built from midrash, commentary and Kabbalah, and meant to be puzzled over rather than read as illustration.

Chagall is in section 9 and the VR *GenesisVR*, made by a Torah studio, in section 13.

## 4. Islamic art

- **al-Qazwini, *The Wonders of Creation*** · Zakariya al-Qazwini; this copy made at Wasit, Iraq · 1280 · Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, Cod. arab. 464, the oldest copy · illustrated manuscript, over 400 paintings and planetary diagrams · *not Genesis: the made order, from the heavens to the earth* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakariya_al-Qazwini)
  The Islamic world's best-loved cosmography: creation as an ordered catalogue, the spheres and angels first, then the earth, its plants and creatures. The Qur'an has six days too (7:54), but I found no Islamic picture of them as a sequence.
- **The angels bow to Adam** · Persian painters, for example a Herat copy of Bal'ami's Persian Tabari (1415–16†) and Safavid copies of the *Stories of the Prophets* · 15th–16th century · Topkapı Palace Library and others† · manuscript painting · *2:7, as the Qur'an tells it (15:28–29; 2:34)* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_in_Islam)
  Adam is formed from clay and given God's spirit, and the angels bow down before him while Iblis refuses. Islam's picture of 2:7 is the moment after: the creature honoured, with a refusal already in it.
- **Sultan Muhammad, *The Court of Gayumars*** *(neighbour)* · Sultan Muhammad · c. 1522–25 · Aga Khan Museum, Toronto, AKM165, from the *Shahnameh* of Shah Tahmasp · opaque watercolour, ink, silver and gold on paper · *not Genesis: the first king, and first man, of Persian myth* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Mohammed)
  The first people, in leopard skins, gathered on a mountain whose rocks are full of hidden faces†: a first humanity in a landscape that looks back.

## 5. Medieval books in the West (1100–1500)

- **Hildegard of Bingen, *Scivias*: the universe as an egg** · Hildegard and the Rupertsberg scriptorium · c. 1165–80 · the original lost in 1945; known from the hand-painted copy made at Eibingen Abbey in 1927–33† · illuminated manuscript · *1:6–8 and 1:14–18* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scivias)
  The cosmos as a burning egg, layers of fire, dark, air and water one inside another, with the stars and winds in them: the vault as something alive.
- **Herrad of Landsberg, *Hortus deliciarum*** · Herrad and the nuns of Hohenburg (Mont Sainte-Odile, Alsace) · c. 1167–85 · destroyed when Strasbourg was shelled in 1870; reconstructed from copies (Straub and Keller, 1879–99; the Warburg Institute, 1979) · illuminated encyclopedia · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hortus_deliciarum)
  An abbess's encyclopedia of salvation, which opens with God, the angels and the making of the world†. What survives is nineteenth-century tracings of a lost book.
- **The Vienna *Bible moralisée*: God the architect** · a Paris workshop · 1220s · Österreichische Nationalbibliothek, Cod. Vindob. 2554 · illuminated manuscript, full-page frontispiece · *1:1–2* · [Facsimile Finder](https://www.facsimilefinder.com/articles/god-architect-bible-moralisee-vienna-2554/)
  The Creator measures a circle with compasses, a lump of unformed matter inside it; the caption says God is creating heaven, earth, sun, moon and all the elements. After Michelangelo's, probably the most reproduced image of the beginning.
- **The *In principio* initial of the Paris Bibles** · Paris workshops · c. 1230–75 · many copies, for example the Met's (ca. 1250–75), Morgan M.494 and M.953, and Cambridge UL Gg.6.15 · illuminated initial · *1:1–2:3* · [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/466297)
  The days stacked as seven medallions down the stem of the letter *I*, often ending at the Crucifixion: the first line of one story that ends at the cross. Made in great numbers for students and friars.
- **The Holkham Bible Picture Book** · English, possibly London, for a Dominican friar · c. 1327–35 · British Library, Add MS 47682 · tinted drawings, 231 scenes · *1:1–2:7 and on (Genesis to the Flood, ff. 1–9)* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holkham_Bible)
  God with an architect's compasses inside a ring, heaven above and the mouth of hell below (f. 2r), the whole book set in fourteenth-century England.
- **The Velislav Bible** · Prague, for the canon Velislav · 1325–49 · National Library of the Czech Republic, Prague · 747 ink drawings with short captions · *Genesis on*† · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velislai_biblia_picta)
  A Bible almost entirely in pictures, with hardly any text.
- **Books of Hours: the *Grandes Heures de Rohan*** · the Rohan Master · c. 1418–25 · BnF, lat. 9471 · illuminated manuscript · *Genesis in the lower margins* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohan_Hours)
  Books of Hours rarely show the days; when they tell Genesis, it's Eden. The Rohan Hours carries an Old Testament cycle in its margins in the manner of a *Bible moralisée*; whether it includes the six days I couldn't confirm†.

## 6. Walls, glass, stone and panels (1100–1450)

- **The Cappella Palatina** · Byzantine-trained mosaicists for the Norman kings · mid-12th century† · Palermo · mosaic on the nave walls · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/designing-norman-sicily/designing-a-visual-language-in-norman-sicily-the-creation-sequence-in-the-mosaics-of-palermo-and-monreale/CD0EACFFEDA52847CF3701F62D9A9805)
  With Monreale, one of the two Norman Sicilian creation sequences in the Cotton Genesis line.
- **Monreale Cathedral, the Genesis cycle** · Byzantine-trained mosaicists for William II · 1180s (some date the nave to the early 13th century) · Monreale, Sicily · mosaic, high on the nave walls · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Christian Iconography](https://www.christianiconography.info/sicily/genesisMonreale.html)
  The days one after another along the nave, with Christ as Creator seated on the globe of the world†: one of the fullest creation cycles in mosaic.
- **San Marco, the Creation cupola** · Venetian mosaicists after the Cotton Genesis · c. 1220–50 · the atrium of St Mark's, Venice · mosaic, 24 or 26 scenes (the sources count differently) in three rings, 6.15 m across · *1:1–3:24* · [AMS Historica (BYZART)](https://historica.unibo.it/handle/20.500.14008/33767)
  The Cotton Genesis's surviving copy: the days in rings round the dome, each day's angel added to the last (five winged figures by the fifth day), the days paired with the seven gifts of the Spirit, and God at rest on the seventh.
- **Chartres Cathedral, the north porch** · the Chartres workshops · 1225–40 · the central bay of the north porch · carved stone voussoirs · *1:1–2:7 and Eden* · [Base Théophile](https://theophile-s-intg.inist.fr/s/theophile/item/68219)
  The creation climbs the arch stone by stone, ending with God making Adam in his image (second archivolt, scene 9).
- **Sainte-Chapelle, the Genesis window** · Paris glaziers · 1242–48 · Sainte-Chapelle, Paris, the first window on the north side · stained glass · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Painton Cowen, The Rose Window](https://therosewindow.com/pilot/StChapelle/w1.htm)
  Where the chapel's eleven hundred scenes begin; very little of its original glass is left.
- **Auxerre Cathedral, the Creation window** · the Auxerre glaziers (Virginia Raguin's "Genesis workshop") · 13th century, probably the 1230s–40s† · Saint-Étienne, Auxerre, window 21 · stained glass · *1:2–2:7 and Eden* · [Painton Cowen, The Rose Window](https://therosewindow.com/pilot/Auxerre/w21.htm)
  It opens with God and the Spirit moving over the waters, then the animals, Adam and Eve and the Fall. The west façade has a Genesis in stone as well.
- **Salisbury Cathedral, the chapter house** · two English sculptors · c. 1263–80 · the spandrels of the chapter house arcade, Salisbury · stone relief, sixty scenes · *Genesis and Exodus* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_Cathedral)
  The fullest Old Testament cycle in English stone to survive the iconoclasts; whether it starts with the days themselves I couldn't confirm†.
- **Orvieto Cathedral, the Genesis pier** · Lorenzo Maitani and Sienese workshops · c. 1310–30 · the façade, first pier from the left · marble relief · *1:1–2:7, then Eden to Cain* · [ArtWay](https://www.artway.eu/posts/lorenzo-maitani-genesis-relief-orvieto-cathedral)
  The Creator walks among the birds, fish and plants as they're made, angels behind him, all held in the branches of a vine.
- **Andrea Pisano, the Campanile reliefs** · Andrea Pisano, perhaps after Giotto · c. 1334–36 · Giotto's Campanile, Florence (originals in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo†) · marble hexagons · *2:7* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto%27s_Campanile)
  The bell tower's history of human work starts with God making Adam: creation as the first of the arts.
- **Bartolo di Fredi, the Collegiata** · Bartolo di Fredi · 1367 · Collegiata, San Gimignano, the upper register under the vault · fresco · *1:1–2:7 and Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiata_di_Santa_Maria_Assunta,_San_Gimignano)
  God over the world drawn as rings†, then the firmament, man, the naming of the animals and Eve, in the lunettes.
- **Giusto de' Menabuoi, the Padua Baptistery** · Giusto de' Menabuoi · c. 1375–78 · Padua Baptistery (UNESCO, with Padua's other 14th-century fresco cycles) · fresco in the drum · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua_Baptistery)
  In the creation of the world Christ sits within the zodiac as lord of cosmic time, under a dome of Paradise crowded with faces.

- **Piero di Puccio, the *Theological Cosmography*** · Piero di Puccio · 1389–91 · Camposanto, Pisa (burned in 1944, restored) · fresco · *1:1–19, the made cosmos* · [Wikipedia (Italian)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camposanto_monumentale)

  God, borne by seraphim, pushes the universe into being as a set of rings, earth at the centre, then the elements, the planets and the zodiac, while on the right Adam and Eve are already being driven out.

## 7. Renaissance (1420–1600)

- **Paolo Uccello, *The Creation of the Animals and the Creation of Adam*** · Uccello · 1420s or c. 1431 · the Chiostro Verde, Santa Maria Novella, Florence · fresco in green earth · *1:20–25 and 2:7* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_and_the_Fall_(Uccello))
  Painted almost in one colour, a green earth, so the creation reads as one substance taking many shapes.
- **Jacopo della Quercia, the Genesis reliefs of San Petronio** · della Quercia · 1425–38 · the Porta Magna, San Petronio, Bologna · stone relief · *2:7, then Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_della_Quercia)
  God takes the hand of a heavy, half-risen Adam. Michelangelo saw them in 1494 and is said to have conceded that his Sistine Genesis was based on them.
- **Lorenzo Ghiberti, the *Gates of Paradise*, first panel** · Ghiberti · 1425–52 · Florence Baptistery, east doors (originals in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo†) · gilded bronze relief · *2:7 and Eden* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Baptistery)
  Adam lifted from the ground, Eve drawn from his side, the Fall and the expulsion, all in one deep panel in perspective.
- **Hieronymus Bosch, *The Garden of Earthly Delights*, the outer wings** · Bosch · c. 1490–1510 · Museo del Prado, Madrid · oil in grisaille on oak · *1:9–13, the third day* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_Earthly_Delights)
  The world inside a glass sphere, the land just greening and no sun or moon yet, all in grey; a tiny God sits far up in the corner with a book, and the inscription is Psalm 33:9, "he spoke, and it came to be".
- **Michelangelo, the three creation panels of the Sistine ceiling** · Michelangelo · painted 1511–12 · Sistine Chapel, Vatican · fresco · *1:3–19* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_Light_from_Darkness)
  *The Separation of Light from Darkness*: God as a twisting body seen from below, his face nearly hidden, dividing the light with his arms. *The Creation of the Sun, Moon and Plants*: days three and four in one, God seen from the front and from behind. *The Separation of Land and Water*, which some read as the second day. The picture of the beginning almost everyone carries.
- **Michelangelo, *The Creation of Adam*** · Michelangelo · 1511–12 · Sistine Chapel · fresco · *1:26–27, read with 2:7* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Creation_of_Adam)
  The two hands that don't quite touch: the most reproduced image in this catalogue, and not the dust of 2:7 at all but life passing across a gap.
- **Raphael's workshop, the first bay of the Loggia** · Raphael, Giovanni da Udine and workshop · 1517–19 · Raphael's Loggia, Apostolic Palace, Vatican · fresco · *1:3–25* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_loggias)
  Four small vault scenes, light from darkness, land from water, the sun and moon, the animals: Michelangelo's God made quick and airborne, and spread through Europe by prints.
- **Tintoretto, *The Creation of the Animals*** · Tintoretto · 1550–53 · Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice, from the Scuola della Trinità · oil on canvas · *1:20–25* · [Cincinnati Art Museum](https://cincinnatiartmuseum.org/art/exhibitions/exhibition-archive/2025/tintorettos-genesis/)
  God flies low over the water and the fish and birds stream out ahead of him like a wake: creation as speed.

## 8. Prints, books and paintings (1490–1900)

- **The Nuremberg Chronicle** · Hartmann Schedel; woodcuts by Michael Wolgemut and Wilhelm Pleydenwurff (the young Dürer had been Wolgemut's apprentice; his part is debated) · 1493 · printed by Anton Koberger, Nuremberg; many copies · woodcut · *1:1–2:3* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle)

- **Jan Sadeler after Maerten de Vos, the days of creation** · Sadeler after de Vos · c. 1580–1600 · Rijksmuseum; British Museum, 1937,0915.33 · engraving, seven plates and a title · *1:1–2:3* · [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Schepping-van-licht-en-duisternis--6f300db72906c9a4e4e26836ab3aeb98)

- **Robert Fludd, *Utriusque Cosmi … Historia*** · Fludd · 1617–21 · Oppenheim, printed by the de Bry family† · engraving · *1:1–3* · [Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/robert-fludd-and-his-images-of-the-divine)
  A black square with *Et sic in infinitum* on each side, the darkness before anything; then, plate by plate, the light breaks from the centre and the Spirit moves on the waters. Three centuries before Malevich's *Black Square*.
- **Johann Jakob Scheuchzer, *Physica Sacra*: *Homo ex humo*** · Scheuchzer; the plate engraved by J. A. Fridrich after J. M. Füssli · 1731 · Augsburg and Ulm; the Wellcome Collection and others · etching and engraving, one of about 760 plates · *1:26–27, with the words of 2:7* · [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/twbrjsc2)
  Adam alone in Eden, framed by eleven human embryos and foetal skeletons, from one the size of a seed to a weeping four-month foetus: man from the dust told through the science of 1731.
- **William Blake, *The Ancient of Days*** · Blake · 1794 · frontispiece to *Europe a Prophecy*; copies in the British Museum, the Whitworth and elsewhere · relief etching, coloured by hand · *Prov 8:27, read with 1:1* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ancient_of_Days)
  Urizen crouched in a disc of fire, setting a compass on the dark: the medieval architect turned into Blake's tyrant of reason. His own favourite design.
- **William Blake, *Elohim Creating Adam*** · Blake · 1795, printed c. 1805 · Tate · colour print, ink and watercolour · *2:7* · [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/blake-elohim-creating-adam-n05055)
  A winged God bends over Adam, who lies spread on the ground with a worm coiled round his leg: the body made as a binding into matter, Blake's dark 2:7.
- **William Blake, *The Book of Urizen*** · Blake · 1794 · a few copies, in the Library of Congress and elsewhere† · illuminated book, relief etching · *a counter-Genesis* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Urizen)
  Blake's own anti-Genesis, in which a fallen god's making of the world and of the body is a disaster: the case against reading creation as order.
- **John Martin, *The Creation of Light*** · Martin · 1824–27 · mezzotint for Milton's *Paradise Lost*; impressions in the British Museum, the Museum of Fine Arts, Houston, and elsewhere · mezzotint · *1:3, through Milton* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Martin_(painter))
  Light tearing out of a black that seems to go back for ever, by the painter of the diorama age: the sublime 1:3.
- **Ivan Aivazovsky, *Chaos. The Creation of the World*** · Aivazovsky · 1841 · San Lazzaro degli Armeni, Venice, the Armenian Mekhitarist monastery · oil on canvas · *1:1–3* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos._The_Creation_of_the_World)
  A figure made of light rises from a torn storm cloud over a heaving sea: a marine painter's 1:2–3, and the nearest painting to our beat 1.
- **J. M. W. Turner, *Light and Colour (Goethe's Theory)*** *(neighbour)* · Turner · 1843 · Tate · oil on canvas · *the morning after the Flood, with Moses writing Genesis* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_and_Colour_(Goethe%27s_Theory)_%E2%80%93_The_Morning_after_the_Deluge_%E2%80%93_Moses_Writing_the_Book_of_Genesis)
  A whirl of yellow light with Moses writing at its heart, painted to test Goethe's theory of colour: light as the subject itself.
- **Julius Schnorr von Carolsfeld, *Die Bibel in Bildern*** · Schnorr · 1852–60 · Georg Wigand, Leipzig · 240 woodcuts · *1:1–2:7 and on* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Schnorr_von_Carolsfeld)
  The German Protestant picture Bible: clear, heroic figures in the Nazarene manner, which furnished schoolrooms and Sunday schools for a century.
- **Gustave Doré, the Bible** · Doré · 1866 · *La Grande Bible de Tours* (Mame, Tours) and its many editions · wood engraving · *1:3* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Dor%C3%A9%27s_illustrations_for_La_Grande_Bible_de_Tours)
  It opens with *The Creation of Light*, God in a burst between clouds; printed from electrotypes in huge runs, it put the beginning into homes all over the world.
- **Edward Burne-Jones, *The Days of Creation*** · Burne-Jones · designed 1870, painted 1875–76 · Harvard Art Museums (Fogg) · watercolour, gouache, shell gold and platinum paint on linen-covered panel, six panels · *1:1–31* · [Victorian Web](https://victorianweb.org/painting/bj/misc/1.html)
  One angel per day holds a crystal globe with the day inside it, and each panel adds the angels of the days before, until six stand round Adam and Eve: the world held as a sphere, one steady figure at a time.
- **George Frederic Watts, *Chaos*** *(neighbour)* · Watts and assistants · c. 1875–82 · Tate · oil on canvas · *the earth forming* · [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/watts-chaos-n01647)
  Giant figures, half rock and half body, rising out of the forming earth: geology painted as bodies.
- **Odilon Redon, *Les Origines*** *(neighbour)* · Redon · 1883 · lithograph album of eight plates; impressions at the MFA Boston, the National Gallery of Art and the Art Institute of Chicago · lithograph · *not Genesis: the origins of life, after Darwin* · [MFA Boston](https://collections.mfa.org/objects/170167)
  *There was perhaps a first vision attempted in the flower*: an eye opening inside a sea anemone. The first eyes, as in our beat 10.
- **Auguste Rodin, *The Hand of God*** · Rodin · modelled c. 1896–1902; the Met's marble carved c. 1907 · Musée Rodin, Paris; the Met; RISD; Philadelphia · marble, in three versions, and bronze · *2:7* · [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/works-of-art/08.210)
  A huge hand rises from a rough block holding a lump of stone from which a man and a woman, curled like a foetus, are coming out: the forming from the earth left unfinished, and the sculptor's hand as God's.
- **James Tissot, *God Creating the World*** · Tissot and assistants · c. 1898–1902 · the Morgan Library & Museum, 1976.22:1; the rest of his Old Testament (369 pictures) at the Jewish Museum, New York · opaque watercolour on board · *1:1–5* · [The Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/110083)

  An old man surges up out of the waters with one arm flung over his head, all whiplash line: creation as a burst of energy, not a throne.

## 9. 1900 to now: art and photography

- **Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis, *The Creation of the World*** · Čiurlionis · 1905–06 · M. K. Čiurlionis National Museum of Art, Kaunas · tempera on paper, thirteen panels · *1:1–31, freely* · [Čiurlionis Museum](https://ciurlionis.lt/activity/exhibitions/exhibition-of-m-k-ciurlions-creation-in-london/)

- **Marc Chagall, *The Creation of Man*** · Chagall · 1956–58 · Musée national Marc Chagall, Nice, the first of the seventeen *Biblical Message* paintings · oil on canvas · *2:7* · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_Marc_Chagall)

- **Salvador Dalí, *Biblia Sacra*** · Dalí · painted 1963–64; published by Rizzoli, Milan, 1967–69 · 105 lithographs after gouache, watercolour, ink and pastel, with the Latin Vulgate · *Genesis 1 among them* · [Bridwell Library](https://bridwell.omeka.net/exhibits/show/prothrobible/limited/dali)

## 14. Looked for, and not found or left out

- **Ethiopian art.** I found no notable Ethiopian picture of the six days; the Ethiopian Octateuchs I could check (British Library, Or. 480 and 482) are decorated with interlace, not scenes. Jules Leroy's *Ethiopian Painting* (1967) is where to look next.
- **Armenian art** beyond the Bible of 1623: no creation cycle confirmed; the Armenian Bible tradition was thinner than the Gospel one.
- **The Romanian painted monasteries, the Athonite narthexes, the Yaroslavl frescoes and Vasnetsov's work in Kyiv.** I searched each and couldn't confirm a creation cycle.
- **Islamic art:** no sequence of the six days found, for the reason in section 4.
- **Dura-Europos** has no Genesis 1, and no ancient synagogue floor shows the days.
- ***The Chosen*** doesn't stage Genesis 1. Season 2, episode 1 has Jesus read the opening of Genesis in a synagogue and, years later, John writing "In the beginning was the Word".
- **Kandinsky and Rothko** painted no Genesis 1. Kandinsky's *Composition VI* is the Flood, and *VII* gathers Eden, the Flood and the Judgement; Rothko's mythic pictures of the 1940s are Greek. Newman is the abstract painter who took Genesis 1 as his subject.
- **The Bassanos and Jan Brueghel the Elder.** The Bassanos painted Eden and Noah, not the days; Brueghel's paradises are Eden. A *God Creating the Sun, the Moon and the Stars* by Jan Brueghel the Younger (c. 1650) I know only from a picture library, unchecked.
- **Lost works known from copies:** the fifth-century Genesis frescoes of San Paolo fuori le Mura (burned 1823) and of Old St Peter's, recorded in seventeenth-century drawings†.
- **Eden, from 2:8, left out:** the Vienna Genesis and the Ashburnham Pentateuch (their surviving pictures start after the creation), the Golden Haggadah (it opens with Adam naming the animals, 2:19), the Dogmatic Sarcophagus (Eve made by three figures), Cranach's paradises.
- **Probably real and notable, but not confirmed to the standard above:** the Moody Institute of Science's *God of Creation* (1940s), the creation woodcut of Luther's 1534 Bible, Merian's *Icones Biblicae* (1625–27), the staged versions of Haydn's *Creation*, Ben Shahn's *The Alphabet of Creation* (1954), James Ransome's picture book of Johnson's *The Creation* (1994), and the Jehovah's Witnesses' *My Book of Bible Stories* (1978), which reached a vast readership.
