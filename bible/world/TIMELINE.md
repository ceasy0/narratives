## 0. The short version

**The adopted model, "text-first," puts the Bible's own numbers first.** It dates the Exodus to 1446 BC (1 Kings 6:1). It also counts Israel's 215 years in Egypt so that Paul's "430 years" runs from God's promise to Abraham to the giving of the Law (Gal 3:17).

| Era | Dates (text-first model) | How firm |
|---|---|---|
| Primeval history (Gen 1–11) | Not dated yet. Decided with the Genesis arc (§3.4). | — |
| Abraham, Isaac and Jacob | 1951–1661 BC | Consistent within the text; no outside record names them |
| Israel in Egypt (Joseph to Moses) | 1661–1446 | Depends on the model |
| Exodus, Sinai and the wilderness | 1446–1406 | Depends on the model |
| Conquest | 1406 – c. 1400 | Depends on the model |
| Judges, including **Ruth around 1140** | c. 1380–1050 | Depends on the model, ± 20–30 years |
| Saul, David and Solomon | c. 1050–931 | ± 10 years |
| The divided kingdom | 931–722 (Israel), 931–586 (Judah) | ± 1–2 years from 853 BC on |
| Exile and return | 605 – c. 430 | To the year, often to the month |
| Between the Testaments | c. 430–5 BC | Exact |
| Jesus | c. 5 BC – AD 33 | ± 2–3 years |
| The early church | AD 33 – c. 95 | ± 2 years |

**This reversed my first recommendation.** The Ruth dossier suggested the late Exodus date, because it lets Rahab be Boaz's mother. Looking at the whole Bible, which is what you rightly asked for, the Bible's own numbers fit the early date much better (§3.1). Under the early date, Rahab is Boaz's ancestor several generations back rather than his mother. Matthew skips generations elsewhere in the same genealogy, so that reading is well within the text (§7).

## 1. How sure can we be?

Confidence varies a great deal by era, and the timeline should show that rather than hide it.

| Confidence | Eras | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchored** | 853 BC onward | Assyrian and Babylonian records name kings of Israel and Judah. Those records are tied to an eclipse in 763 BC, which can be dated exactly. |
| **Well established** | David and Solomon | Counted back from the anchored period using the reign lengths in 1–2 Kings |
| **Model-dependent** | The Exodus through the Judges | Hinges on two forks (§3.1, §3.2) |
| **Internal to the text** | The patriarchs | The ages in Genesis agree with each other, but no outside record names any patriarch |
| **Undated** | Genesis 1–11 | Depends on how Genesis is read (Positions Register items 4–7; FR-01) |

## 3. The forks

### 3.1 When was the Exodus?

| | **Early date: 1446 BC** | **Late date: about the 1260s BC** |
|---|---|---|
| Biblical support | 1 Kings 6:1 says Solomon began the Temple in "the 480th year" after the Exodus, which works out to 1446. In Judges 11:26, Jephthah, around 1100, says Israel has held its land for "300 years," which works out to about 1400. The Judges' own numbers fit too: the book's periods add up to about 390 years, plus Eli's 40 (1 Sam 4:18). | Exodus 1:11 names the store city "Raamses." The real city, Pi-Ramesses, was built by Ramesses II in the 1270s. |
| Archaeological support | Some excavators (Garstang in the 1930s, Wood in 1990) dated Jericho's destruction to about 1400. This is a minority view. | A surge of new villages in the hill country around 1200 BC; 13th-century destruction layers at Hazor and other cities; the Merneptah Stele (about 1208), which fits either date |
| Problems | The name "Raamses." But the Bible updates place names elsewhere: Genesis 47:11 calls Joseph's district "the land of Rameses" centuries before any Ramesses, and Genesis 14:14 calls Laish "Dan" before it got that name (Judg 18:29). The settlement surge around 1200 is answered by Judges 1, which describes a long, partial settlement rather than a sudden one. | 1 Kings 6:1's 480 has to be read as symbolic (12 × 40) and Judges 11:26's 300 as rhetoric. The Judges period has to shrink from about 430 years of stated tenures to about 170 real years. And excavations found little or nothing of Jericho in the 13th century. |

**Jericho is a problem for both dates.** Kathleen Kenyon dated the destruction of the last walled city to about 1550 BC, and radiocarbon dating later supported her. Most archaeologists follow that date. So Jericho fits the early date only on a minority reading, and the late date hardly at all.

**Where scholarship stands.** Many critical scholars doubt that a large-scale exodus and conquest happened at either date. Among scholars who accept the account's historicity, both dates have serious defenders. Kitchen and Hoffmeier argue for the late date; Wood and Merrill for the early.

**Decided (2026-10-01): the early date (1446).**
- **Scripture is the spine (principle 1).** The early date takes the Bible's explicit numbers at face value, and the Judges period fits with moderate overlap rather than extreme compression.
- **Its weaknesses have answers within the text.** Place names are demonstrably updated elsewhere (§9), and Judges itself describes a slow settlement.
- **Its one cost is a gap in the genealogy (§7).** The Bible itself shows such gaps: Matthew 1:8 skips three kings.

### 3.2 How long was Israel in Egypt?

| | **Long stay: 430 years in Egypt** | **Short stay: 215 years in Egypt** |
|---|---|---|
| Support | The plainest reading of the Hebrew text (Exod 12:40) | The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch read "430 years in Egypt *and in Canaan*," counting from Abraham's arrival. Paul says the Law came 430 years after the promise to Abraham (Gal 3:17). |
| The genealogy of Moses | Hard to fit. Moses is Levi's great-grandson (Levi → Kohath → Amram → Moses; Exod 6:16–20), and his mother, Jochebed, is "the daughter of Levi, born to Levi in Egypt" (Num 26:59). With the lifespans given (Kohath 133, Amram 137), 430 years needs gaps in the family line. | Fits with no gaps |
| Genesis 15 | "400 years" of affliction (15:13) | "In the fourth generation they shall return" (15:16). The 400 years are then counted from Isaac's birth, a standard way of reconciling the two. |
| Egypt | Joseph serves under the 12th dynasty (around 1880 BC) | Joseph rises around 1670 BC, when Semitic rulers, the Hyksos, were taking over the Nile Delta. "A new king who did not know Joseph" (Exod 1:8) then matches the native Egyptian 18th dynasty, which expelled the Hyksos around 1550. |

**The Hebrew wording allows the short reading.** The KJV renders it "the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was 430 years": the whole sojourn of the people who ended up in Egypt.

**Decided (2026-10-01): the short stay.** It makes Paul's 430 years exact (Abraham's call in 1876, Sinai in 1446). It lets Moses's genealogy work without gaps. And it puts Joseph in a historically plausible setting, a Semitic official in a Delta ruled by Semitic kings. (Josephus also connected the Hyksos with Israel, *Against Apion* 1.73–105; that's P3.)

### 3.3 The judges: overlap

- **The stated tenures** add up to about 390 years from Othniel through the 40 years of Philistine rule, plus Eli's 40.
- **The text itself shows overlap.** The Ammonite and Philistine oppressions began together, one in the east and one in the west (Judg 10:7). Many judges were regional leaders.
- **The early date leaves about 330 years** (from about 1380 to 1050), so the overlap needed is moderate. The late date leaves about 170 years.

### 3.4 Primeval history (Genesis 1–11)

- **The genealogies** in the standard Hebrew text (Gen 5; 11) put the flood 1,656 years after creation and Abraham's birth at year 1,948. Read as a continuous calendar, that puts creation around 3900 BC under our model. Ussher's famous 4004 BC came from the long-stay reading and other assumptions. The Septuagint's numbers add more than 1,300 years.
- **Some consequences of reading them literally:** Noah dies when Abraham is 58, and Shem outlives Abraham. Jewish tradition makes much of Shem and Eber being Abraham's contemporaries.
- **Your opening scene (FR-01)** shows deep time and an existing human population before Adam and Eve. That can't coexist with reading the genealogies as a complete calendar going back to creation.

**Decided (2026-10-01):** leave Genesis 1–11 undated until the Genesis discussion (Positions Register items 4–7, FR-01, Discussion 001). The series calendar begins with Abraham.

### 3.5 Jesus

- **His birth** came before Herod died. Most scholars date Herod's death to 4 BC; a minority, to 1 BC. Herod killed boys "two years old and under" (Matt 2:16), which suggests a birth around 6–5 BC.
- **Luke's census** dates the birth by a census under Quirinius (Luke 2:2). Josephus puts that census in AD 6, and how to reconcile the two is a known difficulty with several proposed solutions (Positions Register item 10).
- **His ministry** begins "in the fifteenth year of Tiberius" (Luke 3:1), which is AD 28–29 counted the normal way. Jesus was "about thirty" (Luke 3:23).
- **John mentions at least three Passovers** during the ministry (2:13; 6:4; 11:55).
- **The crucifixion** happened on a Friday at Passover, while Pilate (AD 26–36) and Caiaphas (AD 18–36) held office. Astronomy gives two candidate dates: 7 April AD 30 or 3 April AD 33.

**Decided (2026-10-01):** born around 5 BC, ministry AD 29–33, crucified Friday, 3 April AD 33. This takes Luke 3:1 in its normal sense and fits John's three or more Passovers. The main alternative is AD 30, which needs Tiberius's years counted from an earlier co-regency, or a shorter ministry.

### 3.6 Revelation

- **Most scholars** date it to around AD 95, under the emperor Domitian, following Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 5.30.3).
- **A minority** date it before AD 70, under Nero. This view goes with the "preterist" reading, which takes Revelation as mainly about first-century events (Positions Register item 19).

**Decided (2026-10-01):** around AD 95, until Position 19 is decided.

## 7. What this means for Ruth

**Under the adopted model:**
- **When.** Ruth falls around 1152–1139 BC, "in the days of Eli," as Josephus says. That's the early Iron Age, with the Philistines newly settled on the coast. The famine fits the regional drought (dossier §7.1).
- **Rahab is Boaz's ancestor, not his mother.** She married Salmon around 1406, about 260 years before Ruth. Matthew's "Salmon fathered Boaz by Rahab" skips generations, as Matthew does elsewhere: Matthew 1:8 says "Joram fathered Uzziah," leaving out Ahaziah, Joash and Amaziah (compare 1 Chr 3:11–12). Ruth 4:21's list skips the same generations.
- **The rest of the genealogy stays direct.** Boaz → Obed → Jesse → David matches what the story itself insists on: "Obed, the father of Jesse, the father of David" (4:17).
- **There's no Rahab cameo.** But a family memory survives, and it's a strong thread: Boaz's family descends from the Canaanite woman of Jericho who hid Israel's spies. That could be part of why Boaz sees Ruth the Moabite differently. It's P5/P6, and it passes the addition test.
- **Nothing in the dossier's world changes.** It's still the early Iron Age.

**Not adopted:** under the late model, Ruth would fall around 1165–1160, Rahab would be his mother, and she could have made a cameo.
