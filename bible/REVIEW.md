# A fresh read of the whole project

> What I think of *Adonai* after reading every document in this folder in one sitting, and what I'd change. Written at your request on 2026-10-07, the first session with a new model. It's an opinion, marked as mine throughout, and nothing in it is decided until you say so.

**What I read.** Everything: the roadmap, the canon, all six discussions, the Opening's dossier and treatment, the style files, the timeline, and the arc plans. Ruth's treatment, dossier and script I sampled (the first few pages of each, plus the whole beat sheet and plan) rather than read end to end, since Ruth is parked. I also looked at the prototype's frames on your drive and checked your machine, because this session ran on your computer rather than in the cloud.

---

## 1. What's working

**The method is unusual, and it's the right one.** Most Bible adaptations fail in one of two ways: they treat the text as untouchable and make a museum piece, or they treat it as raw material and make something that isn't the Bible. The provenance levels, the dialogue tiers, the addition test and the fit check give you a third way: invent freely, and always know what you invented. I haven't seen a creative project run with this discipline, and it's why your unconventional framings can be taken seriously instead of either waved through or shut down.

**The decisions are recorded in your own words, dated, with the reasoning.** This is rarer than it sounds. Two years from now, when a scene in Isaiah depends on what you meant by "the separation is real as experience," the sentence is there.

**The Opening's treatment is strong.** The thing I'd point to is the spine: one thing followed all the way through, face to particle to proton to photon to cell to animal to body to tree to leaf to the face that steps on it. That's a design, not a montage. The breath across the cut is the best single idea in the project so far, because it makes the Garden's first shot the Opening's last beat and settles, without a word, that 2:7 is both recap and hinge. The six rules in §1 mean the sequence can be built by anyone and still be yours.

**The theology is honest.** The discussions bring the case against, every time. Discussion 007 took your most dangerous idea (malice in God) and found the version of it that the texts can carry, in your own words, without softening it. Discussion 002 did the same for "fallible." That's the project working as designed.

**The science is checked, and the confidence is shown.** The dossier says "low to medium" where it should, and cites what it leans on.

## 2. What I'd change, in order

### 2.1 Write the whole-Bible map before the Garden's treatment

The reason you gave for writing front to end was "so I can know whether to change it based on things I don't see yet." A full first pass of treatments and beats for sixty-six books will take a long time to deliver that view: the Opening alone took three versions over three days for five minutes of screen. The thing that would give you the view in a few sessions is the one document the roadmap planned and never got: `story/ARCHITECTURE.md`, the series on one page. Every episode of the whole Bible in one line each, with its text, its era, its heart, and which discussions it depends on. Then the first pass runs in order with the map as its guide, and you can see the shape of the whole, where the Opening's glimpses would fall, where the bloodline threads run, and which arcs can be skipped or merged, before another hour is written.

**My proposal:** make the map the next deliverable after this round's answers, then the Garden.

### 2.2 Two depths for the first pass

Not every episode needs what the Opening got. I'd define two depths: a **sketch** (half a page: the shape, the text's gaps, the decisions it needs) and a **full** treatment with beats. Most of the first pass could be sketches, with full treatments only where an episode is contested or invented, like the Garden. Otherwise the pass finishes in years, and the point of it was to finish.

### 2.3 Cut the reading load, and give every decision one home

A new session has to read about 150,000 words before it can work, and the same decision now lives in up to seven places (the discussion, FOUNDATIONS, FRAMINGS, the dossier, the treatment, the README, CLAUDE.md, and the roadmap's register). Every new decision costs six edits and risks drifting between them. Three concrete changes:

1. **The Positions Register gets its own file,** `canon/POSITIONS.md`, as the roadmap always intended. The roadmap's §8.2 points to it. *Done this round.*
2. **The roadmap becomes the founding document and stops being edited,** apart from a note at the top saying which sections are superseded by which files. It's 138 KB and most of its live content has moved out already.
3. **One canonical home per decision,** with a "where things live" table in CLAUDE.md: the discussion file holds the conversation and the decision; FOUNDATIONS holds one line and a link; everything else links. *Started this round in CLAUDE.md.*

### 2.4 Design the face now

The Opening is now the exception, so its script and visualization are live, and the recurring face is on the critical path. It's the most reused asset in the series: the first face, the cliff, Adam, Jesus, and every bearer in between. It should be designed once, between male and female so it can lean either way, before any beat with a face is built. That's README item 14, and I'd move it up.

### 2.5 The Garden hour

Fifty-five minutes for forty-two verses will be the most invented hour in the series, in the most argued-over chapter of the Bible, and it comes first. I don't think that's a reason to shrink it; it's a reason to build it from the text's own gaps rather than from dialogue. My first shape is in [Discussion 008](./discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md#round-1--2026-10-07). The one thing I'd watch: the Opening earned its wordlessness; the Garden has about a dozen recorded speeches and will tempt invented ones. The rule I'd set for it now: no invented speech between Adam and Eve that the Opening's grammar could carry instead.

### 2.6 Three theological edges to keep an eye on

- **FR-10, Christ as a bloodline,** is the project's biggest Tension and it's parked until the Gospels. That's right, but it touches earlier arcs: Ruth's ending, David, and the whole idea of a line spreading until it's everyone's, which Discussion 008 now uses for Adam. If the Gospels decide against the literal descendants, nothing earlier breaks, because each earlier use stands on its own texts. Worth saying out loud, so no earlier scene is built to need it.
- **"The devil is what evil actions are"** (your answer this round) needs its own entry before Job and the Gospels, where Satan is a speaking character. Logged as FR-19, with the form that fits.
- **"The first man"** (1 Cor 15:45) read as first in standing is a Tension the project carries on purpose. It should be named in the Garden's dossier, once, and then not apologized for.

### 2.7 Small things

- **Line endings.** The repository is LF and Windows kept reporting every file as changed. A `.gitattributes` fixes it. *Done this round.*
- **Obsidian.** You're reading the project in Obsidian; its `.obsidian/` folder is untracked. Its `workspace.json` changes every time you open a file, so I'd ignore that and commit the rest, or ignore the whole folder. Your call; I've left it alone.
- **The verse-reference checker** (roadmap §13.5) is still worth building once scripts start, and it's a small job.
- **Ruth** is in good shape for a parked first draft. Its one structural issue is length (37 pages against hour-long episodes), already logged.

## 3. On the treatment, since you asked

It's solid, and I'd go to script. Two risks the storyboard will show:

- **Beat 3 carries two full arcs of expression in 22 seconds.** Fear to terror against malice to laughter, then upset and compassion settling to sleep. Real faces need time to be read; a terrified face that's only on screen for a second reads as a flicker. I'd give the storyboard permission to steal five seconds from beat 5, which is the longest abstract beat.
- **Beat 13 carries a running fight, a second fight, a birth, a burial and a river in 36 seconds.** Your rule that nobody stops is what keeps it from feeling like a montage, and it will depend on the view travelling at one steady speed. If it ever cuts, it's a montage.

Your three new notes (the sound of the release, the faces forming in sync, and the four-face rule) are applied in treatment v3.1, with my reading of each. The four-face rule is better than it looks at first: it means the whole smiles on both sides and only the brows change, while the part frowns on both sides. That's Discussion 007's decision drawn as a face.
