## What the text says

Things in Genesis 2–4 that are easy to miss, and that the Garden will turn on.

**The man**

- **"Adam" is the Hebrew word for "human."** Through almost all of chapters 2 and 3 it has the article: *ha'adam*, "the human." It's plainly a personal name only by 4:25 and 5:1. He's made from the *adamah*, the ground: a human from the humus.
- **He's formed outside the garden and then put in it.** "The LORD God took the man and put him in the garden" (2:15; also 2:8).
- **His work is "to work it and keep it"** (2:15). The same two verbs describe the Levites' service at the tabernacle (Num 3:7–8). The garden reads like a sanctuary and the man like its priest.
- **He was "with her"** when she ate (3:6).

**The woman**

- **She's built from his *tsela'*.** Everywhere else in the Old Testament that word means "side": the side of the ark, of the tabernacle, of a hill. "Rib" is a tradition of translation.
- **It happens in a "deep sleep"** (*tardemah*, 2:21), the word used when Abram sees his vision in "dreadful and great darkness" (Gen 15:12).
- **The first human words in the Bible are a poem about her** (2:23).
- **She's named Eve, "life," only after the judgment** (3:20).

**The trees and the serpent**

- **There are two trees, and the tree of life isn't forbidden** (2:9, 16–17).
- **"Good and evil" is a pair that names a whole,** the way "heavens and earth" does. The tree is the knowledge of the pair of opposites.
- **The text never calls the serpent Satan.** It's "more crafty than any other beast of the field that the LORD God had made" (3:1). The word for crafty, *'arum*, puns on the word for naked in the verse before. Revelation makes the identification: "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan" (Rev 12:9; 20:2).
- **God says the serpent's promise came true:** "the man has become like one of us in knowing good and evil" (3:22, against 3:5).

**The turn**

- **"The eyes of both were opened"** (3:7).
- **God arrives as a sound and a wind:** "they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the *ruach* of the day" (3:8). That's the same word as the Spirit over the waters in 1:2.
- **They hide "from the face of the LORD God"** (3:8, literally).
- **"I was afraid"** (3:10): the first fear named in the Bible.
- **Only the serpent and the ground are cursed** (3:14, 17). The man and the woman aren't.
- **God makes them clothes of skin** (3:21): the first death in the garden, and an act of care.
- **They're sent out to the east,** with cherubim and a flaming sword at the way back to the tree of life (3:24).

**Outside**

- **Other people seem to exist.** Cain fears that "whoever finds me will kill me," has a wife, and builds a city (4:14–17).
- **The world of chapter 4 is a farming world.** Abel keeps sheep and Cain works the ground (4:2). Within a few generations there are tents and herds, the lyre and the pipe, and tools of bronze and iron (4:20–22).
- **The New Testament treats Adam as one man with consequences for all.** "The son of Adam, the son of God" (Luke 3:38). "He made from one man every nation of mankind" (Acts 17:26). "Sin came into the world through one man" (Rom 5:12). "The first man Adam"; "the last Adam" (1 Cor 15:45).

## What the science says

- **Our species is at least 300,000 years old** (fossils from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco). The band at your cliff, 55,000 years ago, is already us.
- **A species doesn't begin with one individual.** It emerges in a population over a long time, and there's no first member.
- **We don't descend from a single couple in the genetic sense.** The variety in human DNA shows our ancestors never numbered as few as two at any time in the last half-million years.
- **Genealogy is a different thing from genetics.** Go back far enough and your family tree includes nearly everyone then alive who left descendants. A couple living only a few thousand years ago can be among the ancestors of every person on Earth by a later date, without having passed DNA to most of them. That's ordinary mathematics of family trees (Rohde, Olson and Chang, *Nature*, 2004), and it's the same result your bloodline idea (FR-10) already leans on.
- **Farming begins in the Near East about 11,500 years ago,** herding of sheep and goats a little after, towns after that, bronze around 3300 BC and iron around 1200 BC.
- **The seas rose until about 6000 BC.** The floor of the Persian Gulf was a river valley before that. The Nile delta began forming at about the same time.

## Your idea: the first of a new species

**What's right about it.** Something really does begin with Adam, and "the first man" (1 Cor 15:45) is a real text.

**What pulls against it.** Three things, and the first is your own Opening.

1. **The cliff.** The people there are *Homo sapiens,* and you've made one of them Adam's ancestor. So Adam, who comes after, can't be the first *Homo sapiens.* To be that, he'd have to live 300,000 years ago, a quarter of a million years before the cliff.
2. **Genesis 4.** Adam's own sons farm and herd. Nobody did either until about 11,500 years ago.
3. **The biology.** No species has a first member.

**A version that keeps your instinct.** Adam isn't new in body. He's new in what happens to him: the first human God addresses, gives a charge to, and walks with. That's Stott's *Homo divinus.* In the terms you used in Discussion 001, where minds are layered and the topmost layer is God, he'd be the first in whom that layer is open. (That last sentence is my reading of you, not yours.) And "first man" doesn't have to be a head-count: the same verse calls Christ "the second man" (1 Cor 15:47), and he wasn't the second human being.

## My suggestion

*Mine, for you to push against.*

**Adam and Eve are real people, around the dawn of farming, somewhere between the Tigris and the Euphrates, descended from the band at the cliff. Others live outside the Garden. Their line spreads until it's everyone's.** That's the genealogical view with Stott's emphasis. Why:

- **It's the only view that fits everything you've already decided:** the deep past, the people before Adam, an ancestor at the cliff, and a long gap.
- **It fits Genesis 4** without strain: the farming, the herds, Cain's wife and his city.
- **It fits Paul.** Everyone in his day descends from Adam (Acts 17:26; Rom 5:12).
- **Three clocks agree.** The Bible's own genealogies put Adam around 3900 BC in the Hebrew text and around 5300 BC in the Greek ([TIMELINE §3.4](../world/TIMELINE.md#34-primeval-history-genesis-111)). Farming is in place by then. And the land "watered by a rising stream," where "no one worked the ground" (2:5–6), describes the great river plains just as the seas stopped rising.
- **It gives your bloodline idea a first run.** One line spreading through all of humanity is what FR-10 says of Christ. Paul sets the two side by side: "the first Adam," "the last Adam."

What it costs: "the first man" is read as first in standing, not first in number. That's Tension, and we'd name it.

## Where is Eden?

"A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden, and there it divided and became four rivers" (2:10): the Pishon, around Havilah where there is gold; the Gihon, "around the whole land of Cush"; the Tigris; and the Euphrates.

**The Opening may already be standing on one of them.** If the Gihon is the Nile, "the one that flowed around the whole land of Cush," then the band in beat 13 has just followed a river of Eden north out of Cush. **5. What the Fall adds.**

> It's hard to say, because if the fall was the bringing of spiritual death, that implies the gaining or strengthening of ego. But, what is said in Genesis, 'Man has now become like one of us, knowing good and evil,' seems to imply the loss of ego and gaining of true awareness.

*I think both are true, and the Opening already shows how.* In beat 2 the face's eyes open: that's awareness. In beat 3 the first thing the open eyes see is the dark face: separation, and fear. Awareness and ego arrive in one act, because the one who becomes aware is a part and not the whole.

- "Knowing good and evil" is knowing the pair of opposites, and that is God's own knowledge; 3:22 says so, and God doesn't lie there. God knows the pair and isn't afraid, because he's the whole.
- The human knows the pair, and the first thing he knows is himself: "they knew that they were naked" (3:7), "I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself" (3:10). Eyes open, see the self, fear, hide. That's beats 2–3 in order. An ego is awareness held by a part.

The people at the cliff lived, grieved and buried their dead, but didn't know the pair as a pair. With Adam, humans do. That's the "something major" of your answer 1.

The traditions have been here. Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* IV.38) held that Adam and Eve were children, meant to grow into the knowledge, and that the Fall was seizing early what was to be given. "You will be like God" (3:5) is a part grasping at the whole, and Philippians 2:6 is its reverse: Christ "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped." The Reformers read it as pure loss; the Orthodox read the inheritance as mortality and inclination rather than guilt.

What it means for Episode 1: the Fall plays as the Opening's first forty seconds in two people, with one difference. In the Opening the healing comes straight after the blow. **9. The animals of 2:19.**

*Settled.* 2:19 is read "had formed," as the NIV and ESV take it; the Hebrew verb form allows it, and it keeps 1:24–25 in place. The scene is the bringing and the naming, not the making. **Supported.**

### The check: the Ur and Uruk country

**What you'd be choosing.** Ur, Uruk and Eridu sit within about seventy kilometres of one another on the lower plain, between the Euphrates and the Tigris, near where the rivers reach the Gulf. In the sixth to fourth millennia BC the sea came much farther north than it does now: the mid-Holocene rise brought the shoreline up close to Ur and Eridu, and the coast only crept back south as the delta filled in. So the country you've picked was, then, the mouth of the rivers: a marsh-and-river land at the sea's edge, with date palms, reeds and open water.

**For it.**

1. **No rain.** "The LORD God had not caused it to rain on the land" and the ground was watered by the *'ed* (2:5–6). Southern Iraq gets under 150 mm of rain a year; nothing grows there except by the rivers. The word *'ed* is most often tied to Akkadian *edû*, the river's flood or high water (HALOT): natural irrigation by the rivers' rise. R. Laird Harris and others object that floods come from rain, but the Tigris and Euphrates flood from snowmelt in the Taurus and Zagros hundreds of miles away, so on the plain the water rises with no rain in sight. This is the one place in the Near East where 2:5–6 is a plain description.
2. **The rivers.** Two of the four are certain and meet here. The Gulf theory (Juris Zarins, from the 1980s, using LANDSAT imagery) supplies the other two: the Pishon as the Wadi al-Batin, a dry riverbed that once ran from Arabia's gold country (Havilah, 2:11, read as the Hijaz, where gold is still mined) toward Kuwait; and the Gihon as the Karun, from Iran. "Cush" would then be E. A. Speiser's reading, the land of the Kassites (Akkadian *Kaššu*) east of the Tigris, not Nubia. Genesis 10:8–10 gives that reading a foothold: Nimrod, son of Cush, rules "Babel, Erech, Accad," and Erech is Uruk. *Medium confidence.* It's a hypothesis with serious defenders, not a consensus.
3. **The first city.** The Sumerians put the beginning here. "After kingship descended from heaven, kingship was in Eridu" (the Sumerian King List). Eridu, founded about 5400 BC, is the oldest city in Mesopotamia and the home of Enki, the god of fresh water and wisdom. The Adapa story, a man of Eridu offered the food of life who fails to eat it, is the nearest ancient parallel to Adam. Dilmun, the Sumerian paradise "where the lion kills not," lies down the Gulf. Cain's city (4:17) and the first towns are the Uruk period, about 4000–3100 BC, when the world's first true cities rose, and Uruk is the first of them. None of this proves Genesis happened here; it shows that the people who lived here told the same beginning here.
4. **Three clocks agree.** The genealogies put Adam around 3900 BC (Hebrew) or 5300 BC (Greek). The Ubaid period, about 6500–3800 BC, is exactly when this plain was first settled and first farmed, by irrigation.
5. **The name.** "Eden" may come from Sumerian *edin*, "plain, steppe," the word for this very ground; the older derivation is from a Semitic root for "delight" or "well-watered." Either fits.
6. **A land under the sea.** Zarins's point: the Gulf floor was dry until about 6000 BC, and people who lived there moved north as it flooded and met the farmers coming south. If a garden was lost, a country under the sea is one way a people remembers it. Speculative; P4 at most.

**Against it.**

1. **"Cush" everywhere else means Nubia,** up the Nile. The Kassite reading is a minority one.
2. **The Nile as the Gihon has to go.** You can't have a Gihon from Nubia and a garden by Ur. My recommendation: let the Nile go. The Opening's join to the Garden is the breath and the face, not the river. Josephus's reading stays as a note.
3. **"One river became four heads"** (2:10) describes a river dividing, where these four come together. The usual answer is that "heads" are branches or mouths, which is what a delta is, and as it happens the Opening's last image is a river coming apart like a hand into channels.
4. **The highland view** (the garden at the headwaters, in Armenia) is the other serious candidate and reads "heads" as sources.
5. **No archaeology can find a garden.** The choice is a creative one, made with the text's clues, and that's all it can be.

**What it sets.** The look of Episode 1: marsh and river, reed and date palm, the sea close, lions and aurochs and onagers on the edge of the plain, the birds of the marshes. The date: provisionally the Ubaid period, about 5000–4000 BC, which is also roughly where the genealogies put Adam. The gap from the cliff: about fifty thousand years, in which the line walks east from the Nile. And a fact worth knowing for later: at that date, "the east" of 2:8 and the land of the flood story and of Babel are the same country.

**My recommendation.** Adopt it: the Garden on the lower plain between the rivers, at the sea's edge as it then was, near where Eridu would stand, in the Ubaid period. **Grade: Compatible** overall: Supported on 2:5–6 and on the two named rivers; Tension on "one river into four" and on Cush read as Kassite, both named and adopted on purpose. I'd keep the date provisional until Genesis 4–5 is treated.
