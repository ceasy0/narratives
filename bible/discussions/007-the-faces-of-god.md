## The question

The series opens on a face. You've said it's God's, that all four faces and both of the things that happen between them are "the totality of God," and that the Trinity is God together with the fundamental duality he creates. Does that stand beside the texts, and what would have to be true of the picture for it to?

## The four claims

### A. The face is God's, and God is seen through faces

**What supports it**

- **Humankind is made in God's image** (Gen 1:26–27). The human face is the one image of God that God makes himself.
- **Christ is "the image of the invisible God"** (Col 1:15) and "the exact imprint of his nature" (Heb 1:3). "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9).
- **The Bible's own word for God's presence is "face"** (*panim*). "The LORD make his face to shine upon you" (Num 6:25). "Your face, LORD, do I seek" (Ps 27:8). To be cut off is to have God hide his face (Deut 31:17; Ps 30:7; 104:29).
- **God's face in a human face.** Jacob says to the brother he wronged: "I have seen your face, which is like seeing the face of God" (Gen 33:10). That's your sentence almost word for word, and it comes one chapter after "I have seen God face to face" (32:30).
- **God in community.** "No one has ever seen God; if we love one another, God abides in us" (1 John 4:12). "Where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I among them" (Matt 18:20). "As you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" (Matt 25:40).
- **The end of the story is a face.** "Then face to face" (1 Cor 13:12). "They will see his face" (Rev 22:4).

**What pulls against it**

- **"You saw no form."** "Since you saw no form on the day that the LORD spoke to you at Horeb... beware lest you act corruptly by making a carved image for yourselves, in the form of any figure, the likeness of male or female" (Deut 4:15–16; Exod 20:4). This is the strongest text against opening a Bible series on God shown as a human face.
- **"No one has ever seen God"** (John 1:18; 1 Tim 6:16), and "you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live" (Exod 33:20).

**How it holds.** John 1:18 doesn't stop at "no one has ever seen God." It goes on: "the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known." The classical answer, from Justin and Irenaeus on, is that whenever God is seen, it's the Son who is seen. John says so about Isaiah's vision: "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory" (John 12:41). So the reading I'd name and adopt:

> **The face is God's image: God as he can be seen. It's the Word through whom all things were made (John 1:3), whom Genesis 1:26 says is the pattern for the human face and Colossians 1:15 calls Christ. It isn't a picture of the Father.**

This is also where my v1 default ("the human image") and your answer ("God") turn out to be one thing. **Grade: Supported under that reading; Tension without it.**

### B. Both interactions are God

The large face hurts the small one. The large face heals the small one.

**What supports it.** "See now that I, even I, am he, and there is no god beside me; I kill and I make alive; I wound and I heal" (Deut 32:39). "He wounds, but he binds up; he shatters, but his hands heal" (Job 5:18). "He has torn us, that he may heal us" (Hos 6:1). "The LORD kills and brings to life" (1 Sam 2:6). "I form light and create darkness; I make well-being and create calamity" (Isa 45:7). The Bible refuses to give the wounding to a second power.

**Grade: Supported.**

### C. The terror and the malice are God

This is where I'd graded Contradiction, and where it needs care.

**The terror.** "God is love," and "there is no fear in love" (1 John 4:8, 18). God "does not faint or grow weary" (Isa 40:28). There's one place in the texts where God is afraid: the Son in Gethsemane, "greatly distressed and troubled... my soul is very sorrowful, even to death" (Mark 14:33–34; Heb 5:7), and on the cross, with "darkness over the whole land" and the cry "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Mark 15:33–34). A white face, alone, in the dark, cut off. If the small face is the Son, then your first image is the cross seen before the world begins, and 1 Pet 1:19–20 says the Lamb was "foreknown before the foundation of the world." **Grade: Tension, with that reading named.**

**The malice.** Here the texts are flat.

- "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5).
- "God cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one" (James 1:13).
- "You are not a God who delights in wickedness; evil may not dwell with you" (Ps 5:4).
- "He does not afflict from his heart" (Lam 3:33). The wounding is God's. The malice is not.

**Malice as part of what God is: Contradiction.** By your own rule it can't go in as stated.

**The version that fits is yours.** You said separation is a limit God puts on himself, and that "it's not real. It's just his own illusion. His own perception." If so, the dark face isn't a malicious half of God. It's how the whole looks to a part that has been cut off from it. The texts have this:

- "With the merciful you show yourself merciful... with the crooked you make yourself seem tortuous" (Ps 18:25–26). God appears according to who is looking.
- "The people stood far off, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was" (Exod 20:21). "Clouds and thick darkness are all around him" (Ps 97:2). To the people at the foot of the mountain, God in the dark is terror.
- "Even the darkness is not dark to you... for darkness is as light with you" (Ps 139:12). From God's side there's no dark half.

The picture already supports this reading, in two ways. The malice appears only *after* the face is cut off from the static. And in treatment v2 all four faces are visibly the same face. **Grade under this reading: Tension, adopted on purpose.**

**One reading to rule out.** In FR-02 you made the Father Yin and the Son Yang. If the dark half is the Father and the light half the Son, the picture says the Father wounds from malice and the Son heals. That's Marcion's split between a harsh creator and a kind redeemer, from the second century, and the texts close it off: "the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing" (John 5:19); "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9); "God so loved the world" (John 3:16). I don't think it's what you mean. It's what the picture would say if the halves were persons.

### D. God and the duality make the Trinity, each creating the other

**What supports it**

- **A two and a third that joins them is the oldest model of the Trinity there is.** Augustine: the Lover, the Beloved, and the Love between them (*On the Trinity* VIII–IX). The Spirit as the bond of the Father and the Son.
- **"Constantly."** The creed says the Son is "eternally begotten." It isn't an event that happened once. The Father is never without the Son.
- **Each is who he is through the other.** "Father" is a name that only means something if there's a Son.
- **The world is made as a pair.** "The heavens and the earth" (Gen 1:1) names the whole by two ends ([dossier §2](../story/arcs/01-beginnings/opening/DOSSIER.md#2-your-reading-of-genesis-1-and-how-it-fits)).

**What pulls against it**

- **"Fundamental duality creates God."** You read Genesis 1:1 as God creating the duality. If the duality is a created thing and it in turn creates God, then God depends on something he made. Against that: "Before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me" (Isa 43:10). "From everlasting to everlasting you are God" (Ps 90:2). "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3). "He is before all things" (Col 1:17). God is not "served by human hands, as though he needed anything" (Acts 17:25). **Contradiction as stated.**
- **If the Father and the Son are the two poles,** they're opposites, and the texts present them as alike (John 10:30; 14:9; Heb 1:3). And if they're halves, each is part of God, which is the old error called partialism (FR-02).

**The version that fits.** Put the two-in-one inside God, uncreated: the Father and the Son, with the Spirit as their bond, from eternity. "Begotten, not made." Then the duality God *creates* in Genesis 1:1, heaven and earth, mind and matter, light and dark, is the image of that in created things. God still doesn't depend on the world, and the world is still two because God is. **Grade: Tension, close to Supported in Augustine's form.**

## How other readers have seen it

- **The Tao Te Ching, chapter 42:** "The Tao gives birth to One. One gives birth to Two. Two gives birth to Three. Three gives birth to the ten thousand things." Your sequence, almost beat for beat.
- **Nicholas of Cusa** (15th century) called God the *coincidence of opposites*: the one in whom contraries meet without being cancelled. He was a cardinal, and was never condemned for it.
- **Jakob Böhme** (17th century) held that God comes to know himself only through contrariety, light through dark. He's the nearest Christian relative of your malicious face, and he was accused of putting evil in God.
- **Isaac Luria** (16th century, Jewish) taught *tzimtzum*: God withdraws or limits himself to make room for a world. That's your "God deliberately puts a limit on himself."
- **Process theology** (Whitehead, 20th century): "It is as true to say that God creates the World, as that the World creates God." That's your sentence, and it's exactly where process theology parts from the Bible's "before all things."

## Questions for our conversation

1. **The malice.** Is it really in God, or is it how the whole looks to a part that's been cut off: "his own illusion. His own perception"? If the second, I think the Opening stands as written.
2. **Who are the large face and the small one?** The whole and a part of it? God and what he's made? Or two persons of the Trinity? And how does FR-02's "Father is Yin, Son is Yang" sit on the four faces?
3. **"Duality creates God."** Does God need the duality in order to be? Or is there a two-in-one inside God from eternity, with the created duality as its image?
4. **Does the face recur?** Is the first face Adam's face, and in the end Jesus's? You've called Christ "the blueprint," and the image is handed down by birth (Gen 5:3; Luke 3:38).
5. **Male, female, or neither?** "In the image of God he created him; male and female he created them" (Gen 1:27). Is the first face a man's, a woman's, or one that could be either?
