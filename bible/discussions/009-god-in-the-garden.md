## The question

In the Garden God is more bodily than anywhere else in the Bible outside Genesis 18 and the Gospels. He plants (2:8), forms (2:19), brings animals to the man (2:19), builds a woman (2:22), speaks a command (2:16–17), walks (3:8), calls (3:9), asks questions and gets answers (3:9–13), pronounces sentences (3:14–19), makes clothes and dresses two people (3:21), sends them out (3:23) and posts a guard (3:24). What does the audience see and hear each time, in a series that has decided that God is seen through faces and that his voice, alone, comes in the hearer's own?

## Your view so far

You've called this one of the hardest things in Episode 1 to figure out, and you asked for the discussion. What's already decided bears on it:

- **God speaks through the world and through people** ([001](./001-how-god-speaks.md)). Genesis 1's speech isn't literal. An encounter alone is staged with the world going quiet, the Opening's held note returning, and the words coming in the hearer's own voice, steadier than their own, able to say what they don't want to hear and to be argued with. Provisional until Abraham.
- **God is seen through faces** ([007](./007-the-faces-of-god.md)). The Opening's face is God's image, God as he can be seen, and not a picture of the Father. It recurs: at the cliff, in Adam, in Jesus.
- **The separation is real as experience and never as fact.** God is never actually absent.

## What the texts say

**The Garden is the first sanctuary.** Gordon Wenham and others have shown how much the Garden shares with the tabernacle: the man's work is "to work it and keep it" (2:15), the two verbs for the Levites' service (Num 3:7–8); the entrance faces east, with cherubim at it (3:24; Exod 25:18–22; 26:31); gold and onyx are in the land (2:12) and in the priest's vestments (Exod 25:7; 28:9); the tabernacle's lampstand is a stylized tree (Exod 25:31–36); and the verb for God's walking in 3:8, *mithallek*, is the one for God walking in the camp (Deut 23:14) and among his people (Lev 26:12; 2 Sam 7:6). So whatever the Garden's presence looks like, it's the same presence that later lives in the tent, and the way it's shown here sets the way it's shown there.

**The sound in the wind.** "They heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the *ruach* of the day" (3:8). The word for sound, *qol*, is also "voice": it's the word for what the people hear at Sinai (Deut 4:12, "you heard the sound of words, but saw no form"). *Ruach* is the Opening's own word, the wind over the waters (1:2). The usual reading is "the breeze of the day," the evening wind. So the text's own picture of God's approach is a sound and a wind, and the Opening's first sound is a hiss like wind over water.

**The words, and who answers.** The command (2:16–17) is spoken to the man alone. The questions (3:9–13) are a dialogue with two people, each of whom answers. The sentences (3:14–19) are poetry, spoken to the serpent, the woman and the man in turn. "Like one of us" (3:22) is spoken to nobody on screen. That's four kinds of speech, and 001's device was designed for the first.

**The hands.** "The LORD God made for Adam and for his wife garments of skins and clothed them" (3:21). It's the one act of care after the fear, it's done with hands, and it's the first death in the Garden. Earlier, he "formed" (2:19) and "built" (2:22), both craftsman's verbs.

**Seen, or not.** "You saw no form" (Deut 4:15). "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). Against that, the Garden's language is as bodily as the Bible gets, and the classical answer is already in Discussion 007: whenever God is seen, it's the Son who is seen (John 12:41).

## How other readers have seen it

- **Theophilus of Antioch** (second century) wrote that it was the Word, "assuming the person of the Father," who walked in paradise and spoke with Adam (*To Autolycus* 2.22). Irenaeus and Tertullian read it the same way. This is the oldest Christian answer, and it's your face.
- **Jewish tradition** has the Shekhinah, God's dwelling presence, walking in the Garden, and says the sin drove it up out of the world by stages (*Genesis Rabbah* 19:7).
- **Milton** has the Son come down into Eden to judge them (*Paradise Lost* X), gently, and clothe them himself.
- **Most film and television** uses a voice from above. Nothing in this project has chosen that yet, and 001 ruled it out for Genesis 1 only.

## My view

*Mine, for you to push against.*

**C as the base, D for the animals, and the face kept back.** The Garden should be the first place the audience learns how this series shows God, and it should learn the rule, not an exception. The rule from 001 is that the words come in the hearer's own voice over the held note; the Garden adds the hands, because the text insists on them, and the wind, because the text names it.

- **The command (2:16–17).** The first words spoken in the series. The world goes quiet, the held note comes back, and Adam hears them in his own voice. That means the first human voice we hear is speaking God's words, which is Discussion 001's decision 5 ("the prophet takes part in God, and God is the one speaking") at the very start, and the audience learns the device before it's ever needed again.
- **The questions (3:9–13).** Each hears the question in their own voice, and answers aloud, to the air, to the trees, looking for where it came from. The camera stays on faces. "Where are you?" is a question asked of someone who has just learned to hide; in their own voice it's the voice they're hiding from.
- **The sentences (3:14–19).** This is the hard one, and I'd rather solve it than break the rule. The woman hears her sentence in her own voice; the man hears his in his. The serpent's sentence is heard by both of them, in both voices, slightly out of step. The out-of-step voices would be the first time the device has more than one hearer, and it sounds like a judgment.
- **The clothes (3:21).** Hands, seen. The one place in the hour where God's body is on screen, and it's cutting a hide and dressing two frightened people. If the face is ever to be seen in the Garden, this is the only moment I'd consider, and I'd still hold it back: it's the face they'll see again at the end (Rev 22:4), and the series has a long way to go.
- **The walking (3:8).** Wind in the trees, a sound coming through the Garden, and the two of them going still. The Opening's first sound, as a real wind. Nothing is seen.
- **The sword (3:24).** The Opening's world at full strength for the first time since the cliff: fire that turns every way. The cherubim as the text's own strangeness, not as angels with faces.

**What I'd rule out:** a voice from above (E), and the face as a walking character in Episode 1 (B). The first is the convention this project exists to avoid; the second spends the series' best image before it has earned it.

### What cherubim are, and what the sword has to do with them

**The word.** *Keruvim* is the plural of *keruv*; "cherubim" is already plural, and "a cherub" is one. They are not the fat winged babies of Renaissance painting (those are *putti*, a different thing). The nearest relatives are the guardian creatures of the ancient Near East: the Akkadian *kuribu*, and the winged, human-headed bulls and lions (the *lamassu*) that stood at the gates of Assyrian palaces and temples to guard the threshold. A cherub is a gate-keeper and a throne-bearer, a composite creature, and where it is, a god's presence is.

**Where they appear in the Bible, in order.**

| Where | What they do |
|---|---|
| Gen 3:24 | Posted east of the Garden "to guard the way to the tree of life." Their first job is to keep people out of a sanctuary. |
| Exod 25:18–22 | Two of gold, hammered out of the lid of the ark, wings spread over it, facing each other. God says: "There I will meet with you, and from between the two cherubim I will speak." |
| Exod 26:1, 31 | Woven into the tabernacle's inner curtains and into the veil in front of the holy of holies. So the way into God's presence is guarded by cherubim, exactly as the way into Eden is. |
| 1 Kgs 6:23–29 | Two of olive wood in Solomon's inner sanctuary, fifteen feet tall, wingtip to wingtip across the room, and more carved on every wall and door "with palm trees and open flowers": the Temple's holiest room is decorated as a garden with cherubim in it. |
| 1 Sam 4:4; Ps 80:1; 99:1 | God is "enthroned on the cherubim." They are his seat. |
| Ps 18:10 (2 Sam 22:11) | "He rode on a cherub and flew; he came swiftly on the wings of the wind." His mount. |
| Ezek 1 and 10 | The four living creatures of Ezekiel's vision, each with four faces (a human, a lion, an ox, an eagle), four wings, and wheels full of eyes, with fire moving among them and a throne above them. Ezekiel 10:20 says plainly: "these were the cherubim." |
| Ezek 28:13–16 | The king of Tyre is mocked as "an anointed guardian cherub" who was "in Eden, the garden of God," on "the holy mountain," and was cast out. A cherub in Eden again. |
| Rev 4:6–8 | Four living creatures around the throne, six wings, full of eyes, the same four faces: the series ends with them where it began. |

**The context in 3:24.** The Garden is the first sanctuary (above, "What the texts say"): the man's work is the Levites' two verbs, the entrance faces east, gold and onyx are in the land and on the priest. When the humans are put out, the furniture of the holy of holies is put at the door: cherubim, and fire. From here on, the way back to the tree of life runs through cherubim, which is what the veil says (Exod 26:31), what the Day of Atonement is (once a year one man goes past it, Lev 16), and what the torn veil at the cross means (Matt 27:51). The thread runs to "the tree of life" open again in Rev 22:2, 14. In the map it's the river-and-tree thread ([ARCHITECTURE §8](../story/ARCHITECTURE.md#8-the-threads)).

**The sword.** The Hebrew is *lahat ha-herev ha-mithappeket*: "the flame of the sword turning itself," or "the flame of the whirling sword." Two things to notice. The grammar lists it beside the cherubim, not in their hands: "the cherubim, and the flame of the sword that turned every way." It can be a thing on its own. And *mithappeket* is a turning-over, which is why 008 noted it: the Opening's alternation, as a thing of fire. Readings: lightning, which the Bible calls God's sword elsewhere (Deut 32:41; Ezek 21:9–10, "a sword... polished to flash like lightning"); or a fire that moves of itself as a guardian, as the fire that moves among Ezekiel's cherubim does (Ezek 1:13) and the burning coals between them (Ezek 10:2, 6–7). What it has to do with the cherubim: both belong to the throne. Cherubim and fire together are what God's presence looks like from outside it. So 3:24 says: the Garden is God's house now, with its door shut, and the furniture of the holiest room stands at the door.
