**Needed before:** Arc 18, hour 205 (*The Face*), the series' last episode ([ARCHITECTURE](../story/ARCHITECTURE.md#arc-18--revelation))
**Fit grade:** Compatible, with one Tension to stage around (the face closing its eyes; see below). My first pass.

## Your idea (2026-10-14)

> For the closing scene of the series though, I see some man walking toward a tree, and sitting next to it to watch the sunset (this part is optional as of now, because im unsure who it would be). There would be a voice-over while the camera swaps back and forth between the opening face, and a dark-haired woman that can be seen resting; her eyes are closed but she can be seen getting more and more restless, and you can see the opening face start to close its eyes. While the camera is still zooming in to her from above, the start of Ye's song 'Use This Gospel' starts rising in volume (the beginning of the song would probably be extended). At 0:19 of the song (when the bass comes) is when the camera is zoomed to its closest to the womans face, the voice-over ends with 'Yes, I am coming soon,' her eyes jolt open, and cuts to black. The credits roll as the rest of the song plays, with some stylized credit animations.

You're not sure yet how you want to do it, and Arc 18's plan may change. This file holds the idea and my reactions until we take it up.

## What I think it does

**It closes the loop the Opening opens.** The series starts with a face in the static whose eyes open, and the Opening ends on a held breath. Your last scene has that face closing its eyes as a woman's eyes open. That's an exchange: God's rest, and our waking. The seventh day is the one day in Genesis 1–2 with no "evening and morning" (2:1–3), and "there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God" (Heb 4:9).

**The woman reads as the Bride,** and I think she should be. "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come'" (Rev 22:17); "the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready" (19:7); the new Jerusalem comes down "prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" (21:2). Her restlessness is the waiting of the whole book ("How long?", 6:10). Her eyes open on the promise, which is the reply the text itself gives to it: "Amen. Come, Lord Jesus" (22:20b). Paul has the same image: "Awake, O sleeper" (Eph 5:14).

**And she answers the Garden.** In the Garden, Adam sleeps and the woman comes ([008](./008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md), Eve in the deep sleep as the first glimpse). At the end the woman sleeps and the bridegroom comes. The series would open and close on a sleep.

**Ending on her eyes, not on what she sees, is right.** The map's proposed last image is the face itself: "they will see his face" (22:4). Your version cuts to black at the instant she wakes, so we never see what she sees. I prefer yours. It leaves the audience where the book leaves its readers, at the promise, not past it (22:20), and it keeps the face unseen, as it has been since the Opening.

## The man at the tree

The tree is the tree of life, "on either side of the river" in the new Jerusalem (22:2), open again after the Garden's gate (Gen 3:24). Who sits by it, in my order:

1. **John** (my pick). The old man of hour 202, on Patmos and at Ephesus, the last of the twelve, who wrote these words. He walks to the tree on his last evening and sits down to watch the sun go. The voice-over can be his. It's grounded, and it's earned: he's the witness.
2. **Adam.** The first man home at the tree he lost. Strong as a symbol, but it shows the resurrection, which the book leaves future.
3. **The Garden's ancestor,** the man through whom God speaks ([009, round 4](./009-god-in-the-garden.md)). If Christ is in him (FR-21), the series would end where the Garden began. But that's the one who is "coming," so he can't also be the one waiting.

## The voice-over

"Yes, I am coming soon" is the NIV's wording of 22:20; recorded words of Jesus, so tier A, unchanged. The lines before it can come from the end of the book: "Behold, I am coming soon" (22:7, 12), "I am the Alpha and the Omega" (22:13), "the Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come'" (22:17).

*My suggestion:* John's voice reads, old and quiet; the last line, the only one in Christ's own words, comes in **the woman's own voice** over the held note. That's [001](./001-how-god-speaks.md)'s rule for an encounter alone: God's words heard in the hearer's own voice. She hears it in her own voice, and wakes.

## The face closing its eyes: the case against

"He who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep" (Ps 121:4). If the face's eyes close the way a sleeper's do, just as the voice says "I am coming," some will read it as God going away at the very moment he promises to come. [007](./007-the-faces-of-god.md) already has the Opening's faces settling into sleep, so there's precedent in the series, but the last scene carries more weight. *My suggestion:* stage it as rest, not sleep: the eyes close in peace, the face fully lit, with the held note steady under it, never fading or going dark. The face is closing because the vision is over and what it pointed to is arriving. Graded **Tension** if it plays as sleep, **Compatible** if it plays as rest.

## The song

Two problems, and I'd rather say them now than at picture lock.

1. **The rights.** Putting a released song in a film needs a sync licence from the publishers and a master licence from the label, and stretching its opening means editing the master, which needs their permission too. For a famous track the cost is high and can't be known in advance.
2. **The artist.** Since 2022 Ye has made antisemitic statements in public, repeatedly. In a series whose first half is Israel's story and whose Messiah is a Jew, ending on his song is a real risk: distributors, partners and much of the audience will hear the artist before the song.

*My suggestion:* use "Use This Gospel" as the temp track, its shape and its timing (the long rise, the bass at 0:19 landing on her eyes), which is fine in an animatic that stays on your machine. Then make the final cue yourself in FL Studio to the same shape. That keeps the sound yours (your decision of 2026-10-13) and the scene free of both problems.

## The credits

"Stylized credit animations" could carry the Opening's own visual language: names drawn by the sand, or traced by rotating circles (Fourier epicycles: see [TOOLS §8](../production/opening/TOOLS.md#8-fourier-epicycles)), which suit lettering and line drawings better than anything else in the series.

4. **The face:** its eyes closing as rest, not sleep?
5. **The song:** a temp track with your own cue in the final (my pick), or seek the licence?
