# Adonai

*Working title.* A creative, faithful dramatization of the Bible that expresses your understanding of Christianity. It gets written first as story and screenplay, then produced as a fully rendered 3D animated series, which is the placeholder for an eventual live-action version. It uses modern dialogue so that ancient people feel as present to us as they did to the people who first heard their stories.

This directory holds the planning documents, the Opening's research and treatment, the Ruth pilot's research, treatment and beat sheet, and the first-draft Ruth script.

## The short version

**Modern words, ancient minds.** Characters speak contemporary English, so the audience hears them as people rather than statues. They think, want and know only what people of their own time could have.

**Everything has a provenance.** Every element of a scene is labeled by where it came from: Scripture, history, tradition, scholarly inference, your own conviction, or invention. Every line of dialogue is labeled by how much freedom was taken with it. The words the text records, above all from God and from Jesus, are the default: changing their substance takes a discussion first.

**Make it land the way it landed.** For every scene we ask what the first audience knew and felt that a modern audience doesn't. Then we get that across through staging and story, not through lectures.

**Pilot first.** The proposed pilot is Ruth: small, full of dialogue, rich in cultural detail and low in theological risk. It gets written all the way through before we lay out the full series.

### Now: the Garden (Genesis 2:8–3:24)

All from [Discussion 008](./discussions/008-adam-eve-and-the-garden.md), which has the background and my suggestions. The Garden's treatment can't start without them.

1. **Adam: new in body, or new in what happens to him?** Your idea that he's the first of a new species conflicts with your own Opening: the people at the cliff are already our species, and he comes after them. Your reading of 2:4–7 as a recap points to the second answer: if 2:7 describes everyone, what's new in Adam starts at 2:8, when he's taken and placed.
2. **When and where?** My suggestion: between the Tigris and the Euphrates, around the dawn of farming, descended from the band at the cliff, with the Nile as the Gihon, and with his line spreading until it's everyone's.
3. **The people outside.** Do we see them? Is Adam born among them and then taken?
4. **Eve:** made from his side in the body, or seen in the deep sleep? Is she, like him, from the people outside?
5. **What does the Fall add,** if separation is there from the Opening's second beat? *My guess, from your note on the malice:* the ego. "You will be like God" (3:5) is a separated part claiming to be the whole, and afterward God looks frightening to them: "I was afraid... and I hid" (3:10).
6. **The serpent:** an animal, Satan (Position 16), or something else in your framework?
7. **How God is present in the Garden,** where he speaks, walks, calls, asks and sews. Does your answer for an encounter alone (the held note, the hearer's own voice) apply here, or is the Garden different?
8. **The hour.** About fifty-five minutes for 42 verses. What do you see filling it? Is any of it outside the Garden?
9. **The animals of 2:19.** If the Garden's events start at 2:8, does God form animals then and bring them to the man, or does 2:19 mean "had formed"?
