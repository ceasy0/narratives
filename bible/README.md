# Adonai

*Working title.* A creative, faithful dramatization of the Bible that expresses your understanding of Christianity. It gets written first as story and screenplay, then produced as a fully rendered 3D animated series, which is the placeholder for an eventual live-action version. It uses modern dialogue so that ancient people feel as present to us as they did to the people who first heard their stories.

This directory holds the planning documents, the Ruth pilot's research, treatment and beat sheet, and the first-draft Ruth script.

## The short version

**Modern words, ancient minds.** Characters speak contemporary English, so the audience hears them as people rather than statues. They think, want and know only what people of their own time could have.

**Everything has a provenance.** Every element of a scene is labeled by where it came from: Scripture, history, tradition, scholarly inference, your own conviction, or invention. Every line of dialogue is labeled by how much freedom was taken with it. The words the text records, above all from God and from Jesus, are the default: changing their substance takes a discussion first.

**Make it land the way it landed.** For every scene we ask what the first audience knew and felt that a modern audience doesn't. Then we get that across through staging and story, not through lectures.

**Pilot first.** The proposed pilot is Ruth: small, full of dialogue, rich in cultural detail and low in theological risk. It gets written all the way through before we lay out the full series.

## What I need from you

Everything the project is waiting on, in one place. I'll keep this list current.

### Now: the Opening (Genesis 1:1–2:7)

Each has my default; say "defaults" and I'll go on. The reasoning is in the [dossier](./story/arcs/01-beginnings/opening/DOSSIER.md#7-choices-for-you), and the sequence itself is in the [treatment](./story/arcs/01-beginnings/opening/TREATMENT.md), which also wants your notes.

1. **Is anything spoken?** Genesis 1 is mostly God speaking. My default: no words at all. Narrating the chapter over this picture would expose the mismatch between the order of the days and the order of the shots.
2. **Whose face is it?** My default: the human image ("in our image," 1:26), not God's, and the same face in the static and at the Nile. If you mean it as God's face, we should talk first: a terrified God pulls hard against the texts (Isa 40:28; Ps 121:4).
3. **The taijitu: the drawn symbol, or a shape that evokes it?** My default: evoked. A drawn symbol commits the series to FR-02 before we've discussed it.
4. **Death before Eden.** The sequence shows predation, extinction and tribal killing before the Garden, which pulls against Rom 5:12 and 1 Cor 15:21 unless they're read as human death. Graded Tension. My default: keep it, with that reading adopted on purpose.
5. **Who is the person at the cliff?** A man or a woman, and is this the man of Gen 2:7? My default: left open until the Garden.
6. **An exception to two of your rules** (no montages for passing time; every shot filmable) for this sequence and for visions. My default: yes.
7. **The delta's date.** "Just out of Africa" is about 60,000–70,000 years ago, but the delta only took its fan shape about 7,000–8,000 years ago (I'm medium-confident; worth checking). Which do you want to keep: the date or the view?
8. **The ending.** I'd play the cliff in real time, with no cuts or speed-up, as the seventh day's rest (2:1–3), and have the face take one breath (2:7) before the black. Yes or no?
