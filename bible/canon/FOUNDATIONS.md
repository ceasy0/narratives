## 1. What this project is

- **A creative project, not a historical one.** Its aim is "an accurate and powerful expression of Christianity and, eventually, human history. Or, at least, an accurate representation of my views of Christianity." Historical evidence informs it, but creative judgment drives it.
- **Faithful to the spirit, not word-perfect.** The philosophy is "to capture the nature or spirit of Christianity, not get every little detail/word 100% accurate." The Bible adapting itself for its own readers is the precedent (ROADMAP §2.1).
- **Bound by the texts.** "There shouldn't be anything that completely goes against anything in the texts." Checking this is part of my job.
- **Framed correctly.** "I don't want to change the Bible or its stories, but I want it framed correctly (which I believe it may not be, at least in some areas, due to the amount of time that has passed)."
- **Not a "hidden history."** The spirit must not change. Interpretations can differ from convention, can "expand the view" wider than the Bible, and can take liberties "only where it seems/feels absolutely right to," and only after discussion.
- **Authentic, powerful and fresh.**
- **Audience: you, for now.** "We just decide whether I like it or not."

## 2. Your stance

- **Christian.** You believe Jesus is the Christ, the King.
- **Non-denominational.** "I strive for truth, not the perfect matching of other beliefs or frameworks."
- **Open to other religions as seekers of the same center.** In your words, religions "seek to understand the metaphysical center, or 'God,' and no one can ever actually reach it," so understanding each of them gives a better understanding of God. This project stays with Christianity for now. Stories from other religions and cultures may come later.
- **Never add to God's word.** That's why we go carefully, and why the recorded words are the default (principle 4).
- **Questions over answers.** "I'm not saying I'm right, but I think it's important here to ask many questions and explore the possibilities."

## 3. Non-negotiables

1. **No contradiction of the texts.** Every framing gets a fit check (ROADMAP §8.8). A framing graded "Contradiction" doesn't go in as stated.
2. **Discussion before liberty.** Unconventional framings, liberties, and anything from beyond the canon are discussed first and adopted only if they "genuinely seem right."
3. **Additions must have a reason.** Added dialogue or material "should make sense in context with evidence or the spirit/nature of the story." Nothing is added "for the shallow reason of getting more theology out."
4. **The recorded words are the default.** Changing the substance of a recorded line requires a discussion and a logged decision.
5. **When there's dissonance, we discuss it.** I flag tension between your changes and the text, with the passages, before anything is built on it.

## 5. Your vision of Jesus

*In your words. Status: central to the project. Decided 2026-10-01 in [Discussion 002](../discussions/002-jesus-humanity.md#decision); two sub-questions are deferred to the Gospels, and round 3's two guardrails wait on your answer.*

> The character I see Jesus as is kind and compassionate obviously, but also tough as nails when he needs to be. His humanity, with all of its emotions, fears, and dreams, is one of the most important things I want to showcase with this project. I believe that it is the fact that Jesus has hardships and is human and fallible, yet still able to be introspective about his mistakes and try to be a good man, is what makes his story so powerful and relatable. To me the whole reason there is a Jesus is so that God could meet us where we are — as a fallible creature, and tell us that even though we make mistakes and mess up and go through hell, it will be ok. I feel like Jesus would understand that all the evil anyone does is because of the pain they go through. So in my eyes Jesus is the one who understands most that he is in no way different from anyone else, which is exactly what makes him different.

**Round 1 of Discussion 002 (2026-10-01), in your words:**

> I agree that Jesus did not sin, but do not agree that he never appeared to sin. … To me the mind of Jesus has the correct "pattern" or "blueprints" but lacks the resources to fully build the structure. It is his mind that never sins and he always knows the right path. He just needs to walk it. … Jesus' story is one of falling and getting back up repeatedly. … The story of Jesus is learning how to overcome the pain they bring you, so you can help them. … Jesus understands he's no different from anyone or anything. But he's also aware enough to know what that means. … I think that Jesus can understand how both are true at the same time.

## 6. How God speaks

## 8. How I work with you

- **I flag dissonance.** When a framing or edit pulls against a text, I say so plainly, cite the passages and give a fit grade. I don't silently fix it and I don't silently accept it.
- **I bring the case against.** For every framing, I give the strongest texts and arguments on the other side, as well as the ones for it.
- **I learn from your edits.** After each of your passes, I write down the patterns I see in the style documents and ask about anything I can't interpret.
- **I translate carefully.** Every non-English line comes with its original script, a transliteration, a literal back-translation and a confidence level. Reconstructed languages are always flagged.
- **I write things down.** I don't remember previous sessions. What's in this folder is the project's memory.
- **I report problems as I find them.** You've asked me to tell you about any problem with the story as I work, not only when you ask.
- **I give real opinions.** You've said you prefer a back-and-forth with real opinions to neutral annotation. I'll react, take positions, and move the conversation forward, and I'll mark which parts are my own view.

## 9. How you read Genesis 1

*In your words, 2026-10-03. Status: the reading is shown in the Opening and never narrated. Its fit check is [FR-15](./FRAMINGS.md#fr-15--genesis-1-read-in-order-as-the-history-of-matter); the part about God and duality was decided in [007](../discussions/007-the-faces-of-god.md#decision) on 2026-10-05: God creates duality and doesn't need it in order to be.*

> Genesis 1:1 says 'In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.' I truly believe that, if they knew about the terms and what they represented, the writer of Genesis would have instead wrote 'God created Yin and Yang' because what they were meaning to say is that in the beginning and before anything else, God created fundamental duality. But they didn't know these terms. They knew heavens and earth (which to me seem to mean the spiritual/mental nature and physical nature of the world). … Any duality we can come up with is one that we've seen or experienced, not the fundamental duality… We cannot know or name the fundamental duality, we can only know it through the dualities that it makes… Because duality is fundamental (along with God, making the Trinity in my view. The Trinity exists because God creates fundamental duality, and fundamental duality creates God, constantly.), everything that exists has an opposite.

> I believe the term 'light' is being used here to mean more than it actually means. Going with my panpsychist view, the first separation must've been an experiential one. So I modeled the most basic experiences and feelings - that of connection (light side with dark middle) and that of separation (dark side with light middle).

> The waters represent the two fundamental substances - the two fundamental forces. I think the elementary particles represent the vault. The vault to me is just 'space' for things to happen within. This 'space' only exists with the relationships of the particles within it… the vault was only created through the interaction between both separated waters.

Verse by verse: 1:1 is the heading; 1:2 is the static and the face; 1:3 is the static forced away and the face turning white; everything before the elementary particle is 1:1–5; 1:6–8 is the particles; 1:9–10 is actual matter, solids and fluids; 1:11–13 is matter growing into many forms (confirmed 2026-10-05); 1:14–19 is the stars; 1:20–2:3 is the sea, the flying things, the land, people and rest (confirmed 2026-10-05).

**2026-10-05, on what comes next:**

> I truly think its possible that Genesis 2:4-7 is really just a recap or summary of Genesis 1:1-2:3 rather than a line of events afterwards.
