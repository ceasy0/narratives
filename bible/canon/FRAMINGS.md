## FR-01 — The opening: creation as cosmic and biological history

**Source:** your draft of the Genesis 1:1–2:3 scene. **Arc:** 1.

**Your framing, beat by beat:**

1. TV static flows like a fluid, and an expressionless face emerges from it.
2. Darkness closes around the face. The face turns white and terrified while the darkness becomes a malicious face.
3. At the moment of attack, the image mirrors into a taijitu (the yin-yang symbol) of four faces: terror and malice, discontent and contentment. These represent separation and connection, the two fundamental states of experience.
4. The taijitu spins down into a particle. Three particles form a "trinity," a proton, and an electron joins it.
5. Inside a star, fusion builds deuterium, then helium-3. The photon released travels to the early Earth and into a bacterium.
6. Endosymbiosis, then multicellular life, then early chordates (*Pikaia*, *Haikouichthys*), then onto land: lizard-shaped, then rodent-like.
7. The asteroid hits. A dead animal decays in time-lapse, a tree grows beside it, and a leaf falls.
8. Early humans trample the leaf. A tribe moves north through Africa and ends on a cliff above the Nile delta.
9. Close on one person's eye. Black.

**What fits:**

- **Genesis 1:2 says "face" twice.** "Darkness was over the *face* of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the *face* of the waters." The Hebrew word is *penei*, literally "face." A face emerging from flowing static, surrounded by darkness, is close to a literal picture of the Hebrew. I don't know whether you intended that, but it's a striking match.
- **Genesis 1 is built on separation.** The Hebrew verb *badal*, "separate," appears five times (1:4, 6, 7, 14, 18): light from dark, waters from waters, day from night. Your separation-and-connection duality matches the chapter's own structure.
- **John 1:1–5 has the conflict your draft shows.** It's a second creation prologue ("In the beginning was the Word"), and it says "the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." Reading Genesis 1 through John 1 gives your malicious darkness canonical footing.
- **The opening and closing images mirror each other.** A human face appears at the start, as potential ("Let us make man in our image," Gen 1:26), and a human eye closes the sequence, as fulfillment.
- **Creation speaks** (Ps 19:1–4; Rom 1:20). That fits your belief that God speaks through the world.

**What pulls against it:**

- **In Genesis 1 itself, darkness isn't evil.** God names it "Night" (1:5), and it's part of an order God calls good. Genesis is often noted for leaving out the battle against chaos found in neighboring creation myths, such as Babylon's *Enuma Elish*. A malicious darkness brings that battle back in, unless the frame is explicitly John 1.
- **Is evil as old as good?** Putting malice and terror into the state before creation makes evil as old as good. Some texts support you: "I form light and create darkness; I make well-being and create calamity" (Isa 45:7). Others pull the other way: "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5), and the world is "very good" (Gen 1:31). This is the same question as FR-02.
- **The order of events differs from science.** In Genesis 1, the sun, moon and stars come on day 4, after plants on day 3, and birds (day 5) come before land animals (day 6). If Genesis 1 is narrated over this sequence, the mismatch will show. The draft works under a non-chronological reading of Genesis 1, such as the framework view or the cosmic-temple view (Positions Register, item 4), or with no narration at all.
- **There's no seventh day yet.** Genesis 1:1–2:3 ends in rest, and the draft has nothing equivalent. The final black could be it.
- **There are no words.** Genesis 1 is mostly God speaking ("Let there be…"). How to handle that is the first question in [Discussion 001](../discussions/001-how-god-speaks.md).

**Science notes (all fixable):**

- **Atoms form later than the draft shows.** Electrons joined protons to form atoms about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. That's the moment the universe became transparent and light first traveled freely, which would make a natural "Let there be light" (Gen 1:3).
- **Stars fuse protons, not atoms.** Inside stars, hydrogen is ionized, so fusion is bare protons colliding. Your fusion steps are right: proton plus proton gives deuterium, and deuterium plus proton gives helium-3 and a gamma-ray photon.
- **One photon is poetic license.** A photon made in the Sun's core takes tens of thousands of years or more to work its way out, absorbed and re-emitted countless times. Following "one photon" is license, and worth taking. Some alphaproteobacteria do photosynthesize, so that beat holds.
- **Endosymbiosis came much later than the fiery early Earth.** The bacterium-swallowed-by-archaeon event happened roughly 1.5–2 billion years ago. The host is thought to have been an Asgard archaeon, which matches your "ancestral archaeon."
- **Our ancestors weren't lizards.** The mammal line split from the reptile line around 320 million years ago. The early forms were lizard-*shaped*. Early mammals were more shrew-like than rodent-like.
- **"Jungle" should mostly be savanna.** East Africa was mostly savanna and woodland. The Sahara was green during humid periods, and the Nile corridor is one of the proposed routes out of Africa.

**Positions this implies:**

- **Item 4:** how to read Genesis 1.
- **Item 5:** Adam and Eve. Your Adam and Eve section follows an existing human population. Gen 4:14–17 hints at other people (Cain fears being killed, takes a wife and builds a city). Joshua Swamidass's *The Genealogical Adam and Eve* (2019) argues that a recent couple could be genealogical ancestors of everyone, which connects to your bloodline idea.
- **Item 7:** the flood.
- **Possibly where Eden was.** Gen 2:13's Gihon "flows around the whole land of Cush," and Josephus identified it with the Nile, which fits your Nile ending. But Gen 2:14 names the Tigris and the Euphrates.

**About the tool list in the draft:** it came from an earlier AI session. Tools change monthly, so we'll choose them in Phase 6 (ROADMAP §15.5). The abstract opening is a strong candidate for procedural animation.

**First-pass grade:** Compatible under a non-chronological reading of Genesis 1, especially with John 1 as the frame. Tension on the malicious darkness.

**Questions:**
- Is God's speech heard over this sequence?
- Whose face is it?
- Is the taijitu shown as the recognizable symbol, or as an image that evokes it?

## FR-02 — God, Yin and Yang as the Trinity

**Source:** your conversation notes. **Arcs:** every depiction of God, and the opening.

**Your framing:** duality (Yin and Yang) and the whole (God) are equally fundamental. The Father is Yin, the Son is Yang, and the Holy Spirit is God, the whole.

**What fits:**
- Augustine described the Spirit as the bond of love between the Father and the Son, which is close to the Spirit as the whole that holds the two together.
- Distinction within unity is the core of Trinitarian thought.
- Genesis 1 is built on separations, and Isaiah 45:7 says God forms both light and darkness.

**What pulls against it:**
- The texts present each person as fully God: "the Word was God" (John 1:1), "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30), "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). The Father and the Son are presented as alike, not as opposites.
- If they are halves, they are parts of God. The ancient name for that view is partialism.
- "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5).

**First-pass grade:** Tension. It shapes the imagery of the opening and Discussion 001.

## FR-03 — Mary and Joseph: the conception

**Source:** your study notes on Matthew 1:18–25, revised 2026-10-01. **Arc:** 16.

**Your framing (revised 2026-10-01):**
- Mary conceived Jesus without sex, so she remained a virgin.
- But the child still came from both "vital opposites": one of Mary's eggs was fertilized by Joseph.
- Mary had other children afterward.
- The "village's version," that they conceived before marriage, is how the community saw it, and that approach works for you "especially if supported."

*(Your original framing, that they conceived together through sex before marriage, was graded Contradiction. The revision replaces it.)*

**What fits:**
- **Virginity is kept,** so Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:23 fit, and so does Mary's "I do not know a man" (Luke 1:34).
- **The texts never describe how the conception happened,** only who brought it about: "from the Holy Spirit" (Matt 1:18, 20), and "the Holy Spirit will come upon you" (Luke 1:35).
- **The strongest case for your view.** The New Testament insists Jesus is descended from David "according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3; also 2 Tim 2:8; Acts 2:30, "of the fruit of his loins"; Acts 13:23). That echoes 2 Samuel 7:12's promise, which is in your notes, of an heir "who shall come from your body." Matthew traces that descent through Joseph. If Jesus had no biological link to Joseph, his descent from David has to come some other way. The traditional answers are:
  - Mary was also of David's line. This is never stated outright. Luke 1:27 is grammatically ambiguous about whether "of the house of David" describes Joseph or Mary.
  - Joseph's legal adoption of Jesus was enough.
- **In your own framework, this version has a striking shape.** The Spirit, as the whole, joins the two opposites, man and woman. That mirrors your Trinity (FR-02), where the Spirit is the bond that unites the two.
- **Mary's other children** fit Matthew 1:25 ("until") and Mark 6:3 (Positions Register, item 17).

**What pulls against it:**
- **Luke 3:23:** Jesus was "the son, *as was supposed*, of Joseph." In your version he really is Joseph's son biologically, so the supposition would be true, and Luke's qualifier becomes hard to explain. This is the strongest text against it.
- **Matthew 1:16** still breaks the genealogy's "X fathered Y" pattern at Joseph ("of whom," meaning Mary, "was born Jesus"). It reads like a deliberate statement that Joseph didn't father him.
- **Matthew 1:19–20.** Joseph doesn't know where the child came from, and the angel's explanation is "from the Holy Spirit," not "yours." That's compatible with your version only if Joseph never learns his part.
- **No ancient tradition I know of holds this exact view.** The early Jewish-Christian Ebionites held that Jesus was Joseph's son by ordinary means, which was your original version (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.2), and the wider church rejected them.
- **It's still a miracle.** In that world there was no way to conceive without some sexual contact, so your version is itself a miracle: the Spirit carrying Joseph's line into Mary. That's fine. It just means the question isn't whether there was a miracle but which one.

**First-pass grade:** **Tension**, up from Contradiction.

**On screen (proposed):**
- **Dialogue and narration stay with the text: "from the Holy Spirit."** No line states how it happened, and Mary never "knew a man."
- **Your view lives where the text is silent, as P5.** For example, Jesus can visibly resemble Joseph. Nothing in the text rules that out, and it quietly holds your belief without asserting it. It also feeds the village's suspicion, which helps the drama.
- **The village's version:** in Nazareth's eyes, they conceived before marriage. The support for it:
  - Mark 6:3 calls Jesus "the son of Mary," which is unusual for a man whose father was known.
  - Matthew's parallel changes that to "the carpenter's son" (Matt 13:55), possibly because Mark's wording sounded like an insult.
  - His opponents say, "We were not born of sexual immorality" (John 8:41).
  - By the late 2nd century, a pagan critic named Celsus was repeating a story that Jesus was illegitimate (reported by Origen, *Against Celsus* 1.28, 1.32).

  These are hints, not proof, but they're enough to stage it.

**Your follow-up (2026-10-01):** you'd expect some reason or conflict that made sex between Mary and Joseph impossible, with the Holy Spirit carrying Joseph's seed into Mary. You're fine with this for now and will raise any problems later.

**The text already supplies the reason.** No invention is needed:
- They were betrothed and hadn't yet "come together" (Matt 1:18). Betrothed couples didn't live together until the wedding.
- Right after the annunciation, Mary goes "with haste" to Elizabeth in the hill country of Judah and stays about three months (Luke 1:39, 56), days away from Nazareth.
- When she comes back, the pregnancy is discovered (Matt 1:18), and Joseph has "no union with her" until the birth (Matt 1:25).

**Status:** your view is adopted provisionally (P5 where the text is silent). The on-screen approach as proposed: the dialogue stays with the text, the resemblance is held quietly, and the village's rumor is staged. Confirm before the Gospels arc.

## FR-04 — The Spirit as the true Law

**Source:** your study notes on Matthew 5:17–20, and the water image. **Arcs:** 5 and 16.

**Your framing:** written laws express ideals in a "sufficient situation." When circumstances change, the Spirit routes around obstacles the way water does, taking the path of least resistance. It fulfills the law rather than abolishing it.

**What fits, strongly:**
- **Mark 10:5.** Jesus says Moses allowed divorce "because of your hardness of heart." That's a law fitted to circumstances, in Jesus's own words.
- **Mark 2:23–28 and Matthew 12:1–8.** David ate the consecrated bread (1 Sam 21); "the Sabbath was made for man"; "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (Hos 6:6).
- **The law written on the heart** (Jer 31:33; 2 Cor 3:6; Rom 7:6; Gal 5:18).
- **The Torah itself distinguishes killings by context** (Exod 22:2; Num 35).

**What pulls against it:**
- "Not one iota" will pass from the law, and whoever relaxes the least commandment is least in the kingdom (Matt 5:18–19). Jesus's "fulfilling" in Matthew 5 deepens the demand, extending it to anger and lust.
- Paul's account of why the law was given (Gal 3:19, 24) differs from "because they could now follow it."

**First-pass grade:** Supported as a principle. The fit depends on how it's applied: it fails for FR-03, but it's strongly supported for Jesus *appearing* to sin ([Discussion 002](../discussions/002-jesus-humanity.md)).

## FR-06 — The Beatitudes through water and the Tao

**Source:** your study notes on Matthew 5:3–12. **Arc:** 16.

**Your framing:**
- "Poor in spirit" means the empty mind, through which the Spirit flows.
- The Beatitudes are about duality and reversal.
- The meek are like water. The pure in heart are like clear water. Peacemakers are like still water.
- The persecuted represent "the feminine": struck, but never dying.

**What fits:**
- The Beatitudes really are reversals (Luke 1:52–53; 6:20–26).
- The Tao Te Ching parallels are real: chapters 8 ("the highest good is like water"), 15 (muddy water clears when left still) and 78 (nothing is softer than water, yet nothing overcomes the hard better).
- Meister Eckhart read "poor in spirit" almost exactly as you do: to want nothing, know nothing and have nothing (his Sermon 52).
- It fits Christ's self-emptying in Philippians 2:7.

**What pulls against it:**
- "Without belief" sits uneasily with the Gospels' call to "repent and believe" (Mark 1:15).
- Luke's version reads "blessed are you who are poor" (6:20), meaning material poverty, which the empty-mind reading can skip past.

**First-pass grade:** Compatible as an interpretive layer. The words stay Tier A. The framing shapes delivery and imagery, and since the sermon happens beside the Sea of Galilee, water is already in the frame.

## FR-08 — Marriage to one's relative opposite

**Source:** your study notes on Matthew 5:31–32. **Arcs:** **7 (Ruth, the pilot)**, 2 (Jacob), 9 (David) and 16.

**Your framing:**
- Each person has one "relative opposite," the only person they can truly be married to.
- Marriage or children with anyone else amounts to adultery.
- Legal marriage is an administrative help.
- Only the two people involved can tell whether they're truly married or truly divorced.

**What fits:**
- **Genesis 2:18's "a helper fit for him"** is literally *'ezer kenegdo*: "a helper corresponding to him," or "opposite him." Your "relative opposite" is in the Hebrew.
- **"What God has joined together"** (Matt 19:6): the joining is God's, not the state's.

**What pulls against it:**
- Widows are free to remarry (Rom 7:2–3; 1 Cor 7:39).
- The law required levirate marriage, where a widow marries her dead husband's brother (Deut 25:5–10).
- There is no marriage in the resurrection (Matt 22:23–30).

**Why it's needed before the pilot:** Ruth marries Mahlon, is widowed, and then marries Boaz. Under your framing, which man was her true opposite, and what was the first marriage? Other places it applies:
- **Jacob, Rachel and Leah** fit it strikingly. Jacob loves Rachel, Leah is imposed on him, and suffering follows.
- **David's marriages.**
- **The Samaritan woman's five husbands** (John 4:16–18).

**First-pass grade:** Supported (the idea of a corresponding partner); Tension (the claim that there is only one).

### Ruth: who is her opposite? (2026-10-01)

*Your theory is a working theory, and it's recorded as one. You asked me to consider it, so this is my reading.*

If the theory is true, **Boaz is much the likelier candidate.** Ruth is also one of the stories where the theory fits best:

1. **The text pairs them in its own words.** Boaz is introduced as *ish gibbor hayil*, "a man of worth" (2:1). He later calls Ruth *eshet hayil*, "a woman of worth" (3:11). It's the same word, and Ruth is the only individual woman the Hebrew Bible calls that. In Hebrew Bibles, Ruth comes right after Proverbs, which ends with its poem about the *eshet hayil*.
2. **They mirror each other's kindness.** Both are defined by *hesed*, loyal kindness (2:20; 3:10). The "wings" Boaz blesses her under (2:12) become the "wing" of his own garment that she asks him to spread over her (3:9).
3. **They're opposite in every circumstance and alike in character.** Older and younger (3:10), rich and destitute, landowner and gleaner, Israelite and Moabite, insider and foreigner. That's your "two sides of one coin" almost exactly.
4. **"Chance."** She "happened" to come to his field (2:3). The Hebrew is literally "her chance chanced upon" it. In your framework, that's water finding its way.
5. **Fruitfulness.** Ten years in Moab produce no child (1:4–5). With Boaz, "the LORD gave her conception" (4:13), one of only two places where the narrator says God acted. That line leads to David and to Jesus.
6. **Mahlon is almost absent.** He has no lines and no character, and his name sounds like "sickness." The final genealogy skips him: "Boaz fathered Obed" (4:21; Matt 1:5).

**What pulls against this reading:**
- Boaz marries Ruth explicitly "to perpetuate the name of the dead," meaning Mahlon's, "in his inheritance" (4:10). The text frames the second marriage as serving the first.
- The text honors the first marriage. Naomi blesses both daughters-in-law for the kindness they showed "the dead" (1:8), and Boaz praises everything Ruth has done "since the death of your husband" (2:11).

**How it could play on screen (my suggestion):**
- **The theory stays unspoken (P5).** No character states it.
- **The marriage to Mahlon is real and loving on Ruth's side,** since her loyalty to his family drives the whole story. But it's unfinished: he's ill, and there's no child.
- **In your water image, the first marriage is the barrier the water flows around.** It's the route that brings her to Naomi, to Bethlehem, to Israel's God and to Boaz, so nothing is wasted.
- **Boaz and Ruth are staged as mirrors:** the shared word "worth," the wing, the field.

**Status:** **Confirmed** 2026-10-01. Boaz is Ruth's opposite, and the theory stays unspoken "unless it feels right." Applied in the [Ruth dossier](../story/arcs/07-ruth/DOSSIER.md#11-framings-and-decisions-that-apply) and staged in the [treatment](../story/arcs/07-ruth/TREATMENT.md#4-threads).

## FR-09 — Children as the parents' next life; hell as a long mindset

**First-pass grade:**
- **Supported** if "next life" means what passes from parents to children.
- **Contradiction** if it means literal reincarnation.
- **Tension** on hell.

**Question:** do you mean "next life" literally or metaphorically?

## FR-12 — The messianic prophecies

**Source:** your study notes. Your collection includes Gen 22:15–18 and 49:8–12, 2 Sam 7:4–17, Isa 7:13–25, 9:1–7 and 11, Jer 23:1–8, and Luke 1:26–38 and 1:67–80.

**First-pass grade:** Supported. This is the traditional Christian reading.

**Two details that bear on Discussion 002:**
- **2 Samuel 7:14.** You included the whole verse: "When he commits iniquity, I will discipline him." Hebrews 1:5 quotes the first half about Jesus and stops before this line. The original applied it to Solomon and David's line.
- **Isaiah 7:15–16.** The child must come to know how "to refuse the evil and choose the good." That's a picture of the Messiah developing over time, which fits your "pattern first, resources later."

**Your reading (2026-10-01):** both passages are part of your picture of the Messiah. He never willfully commits iniquity; he only appears to. As a child he has to learn to refuse evil and choose good. Isaiah 7:15–16, read that way, is Supported. 2 Samuel 7:14b, read as "appears to commit iniquity," is Tension: it works as typology, but not as a plain statement about Jesus ([Discussion 002](../discussions/002-jesus-humanity.md#decision)).

Jewish tradition reads most of these passages as being about kings of their own time or a future human king. That's worth knowing when we fill in the Positions Register.

## FR-13 — Matthew's genealogy as Jesus's paternal line

**What pulls against it:**
- Matthew 1:16 and Luke 3:23 (see FR-03).
- The two genealogies diverge after David: Matthew goes through Solomon and Luke through Nathan.

**The traditional solutions:**
- Matthew gives Joseph's legal, royal line and Luke gives Mary's. This view appears from the 1400s on.
- Both are Joseph's line, connected through a levirate marriage (Julius Africanus, around AD 220).

**First-pass grade:** Tension. Decide it together with FR-03.

## FR-14 — The six religions as mirrors

**Your framing:** Judaism and Hinduism, Christianity and Buddhism, and Islam and Taoism mirror each other across West and East.

This lies mostly beyond the Bible's window and feeds Discussion 004. Two doorways inside the window you might like:

- **The Magi** (Matt 2:1) were most likely Persian priests: foreign seekers who follow a star to Christ.
- **The Exile and Return arcs** fall in what Karl Jaspers called the Axial Age. Buddha, Confucius, Laozi and the early Greek philosophers were alive then, so the series could glimpse that wider world.
