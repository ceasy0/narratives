# Voices

> How each character speaks: their rhythm, their words, and what they'd never say. Started with Ruth, and it grows with every arc.

**Status:** v1, 2026-10-02, drawn from the first-draft Ruth script and your edits to the treatment and the sample scene. Your rewrite of the script will be the real test. Whatever you change in a character's lines, I'll record here.

The baseline for everyone is setting 2: plain modern English (ROADMAP §5.2), with the patterns in [`STORYTELLING.md`](./STORYTELLING.md). This file records only what's particular to each voice.

---

## Ruth

**Accent:** Moabite (ROADMAP §5.7). The accent is the only thing foreign about her English.

**How she talks:** few words, plain and direct, and she means every one. She asks real questions ("Then how do they pray to him?" "Who are you talking to?" "Where is he?"). She doesn't explain herself; the one long speech she gives is the oath. Her humor is dry and short ("It wouldn't be funny"). With Boaz she's formal at first, then bolder than she was told to be ("You're a redeemer").

**She never:** argues, pleads, or makes a speech about her feelings. When she's moved, she acts: bread at a grave, a hand on a sleeve, stones cleared.

**Lines that set her voice:** "It's supposed to." "They're my family." "Give her back to my mother." "It wouldn't be funny."

## Naomi

**Accent:** Bethlehem's.

**How she talks:** a little more formal than Ruth (DOSSIER §12, decision 6), and much quicker. She bargains, she jokes drily, and she says exactly what she thinks of God to his face. Her grief comes out sharp rather than soft: "Am I going to have more sons for you to marry?" Her best lines are short: "Two." "You said two." "Wait, my daughter."

**Her range is her arc:** warm and teasing in the prologue; hard and quick in the bargain; flat and bitter at the well; dry again ("His young women, my daughter") as she comes back; a planner in Act Three.

**Names:** she says "my daughter" or "my daughters," never "Ruth," until 3:1. After that, still mostly "my daughter," as in the text.

**She never:** asks for pity, or hides her anger at God behind piety.

## Boaz

**Accent:** Bethlehem's.

**How he talks:** courteous and a little formal, an older man's speech. He blesses people as a matter of course: his first words in both the prologue and Act Two are blessings. He gives orders quietly and briefly ("You." "Take it."). He's warmer in actions than in words.

**Names:** "my daughter" to Ruth twice at most, where the age gap matters (3:10). He calls Elimelech by name.

**He never:** flirts, presses, or talks about himself. The one time he says what he thought ("You haven't gone after the young men"), it comes out as praise of her.

## Elimelech

**How he talks:** short, firm sentences, like a man holding a door shut. "Enough." "Yes." "None of us were there. We've come to work." "A full measure. That was the bargain." He doesn't explain his decisions to his family, and he doesn't complain.

## Mahlon and Chilion

**Mahlon:** the older brother, irritable, dry. "Every day. You say it every single day." "It wasn't funny the first time." As he dies, his humor is the last thing left: "He'd have a joke for this."

**Chilion:** the joker. A bit for every occasion ("House of bread"), including the ones where it can't work ("At least he doesn't have to—").

## Orpah

**Accent:** Moabite.

**How she talks:** a talker who can't stand a silence. Quick questions, opinions about boys, delight in gossip ("We'll know everybody's business in a week"). When it matters, she's brief: "Mother." "Ruth. Come with me."

**She's never** written as the one who failed. She does what Naomi asks.

## Ruth's family

- **Her mother:** practical love. She shows it by doing (re-tying the knot), and says the hardest thing straight out: "Their god took your husband." Sincere toward Chemosh: she talks to him plainly, "the way you'd speak to an elder you trust."
- **Her father:** a trader's mind. "I've let cheap hands onto our land." He respects anyone who won't be cheated ("Israelites"), and he can't say goodbye.

## Bethlehem

- **Naomi's friend:** warm, plain, direct. "Moab, Naomi?" "Come back." "Naomi?" She says the name on purpose at the end.
- **The old elder:** formal and brief, a voice like a dry branch. Legal formulas: "Witness it."
- **The neighbor:** shrewd, not cruel. A single chuckle and a gesture do most of his talking.
- **The foreman:** young, careful, a little defensive with the boss.
- **The gleaners:** the young one sharp and loud; the old one never looks up from her work and always has the last word.
- **Mr. So-and-so:** solid, careful, a father first. "Land is land."

## Accents

The Moabite accent is designed once and kept across the series (ROADMAP §5.7). It mustn't be a real modern accent. Israelites from different tribes may get regional colors later (the Ephraimites' "Sibboleth," Judg 12:6, is the Bible's own proof that they had them).
