# Storytelling

> How you want scenes to play, learned from your notes. Every treatment, scene card and script starts from this file.

**Status:** started 2026-10-01, from your first round of notes on the Ruth treatment. It grows with each round. When a note is specific to one story, it stays in that story's files; when it's a pattern, it goes here.

---

## On screen

- **No title cards or quote cards,** for now. Scenes fade in from black and out to black.
- **No montages for passing time.** Cut straight to the later moment and let the change show. For a long jump, fade to black and caption it: *Ten years later.*
- **It's headed for live action.** The 3D animation is the placeholder, so write every shot as something a camera and real actors could do (ROADMAP §15). Expect moments of bright, vibrant color and some scenes that need advanced effects.

## People

- **Real family dynamics.** Keep the humor and warmth, and add the friction a modern eye recognizes: brothers who annoy each other, parents who tell them to settle down. (Ruth: Chilion's "House of bread," Mahlon's irritation, Elimelech's "Enough.")
- **Group scenes need real interaction.** At departures, arrivals and gatherings, people talk to one another: friends, neighbors, officials, kin. They don't just look on.
- **Follow more than one person.** Give the other leads scenes from their own point of view, with their own homes, families and lives. (Ruth gets her own scenes in Moab before she joins Naomi's story.)
- **Big choices need visible reasons.** When a character does something extraordinary, build up what's pulling her before she does it. (Ruth's loyalty is earned across Act One.)
- **Let faces carry layers.** Many feelings can pass across one face in one line. Your example: Naomi struggles to get her farewell out, tearing up, with calculation, pain, exhaustion and acceptance all passing over her face. Her characters are colorful, layered and determined.
- **Small acknowledgments matter.** A slight, direct nod can say more than a speech. (Naomi's nod after Ruth's oath.)

## Peoples and cultures

- **No people is all good or all bad.** Where history gives a people reason for hostility or coldness, show it, alongside the kind and curious among them. (Moab's suspicion and its old grudge, a hard employer, a sincere mother, a girl who brings bread.)
- **When cultures differ, let characters notice.** Let them ask and explain the way people really would, briefly and in character. (The boys ask why the Moabites cut themselves in mourning; Naomi answers in a sentence.)
- **Check the custom before staging it.** For example: would they really mourn an outsider the same way as one of their own? (No, so nobody comes to Elimelech's burial except Ruth.)

## Dialogue

- **It must sound real, not scripted.** People interrupt, trail off, joke at the wrong moment and don't say everything they mean.
- **Every scene should flow into the next.** Look at the transition as well as the scene.
- **God's name is used sparingly.** Characters say "God" or "the Lord." "Yahweh" is kept for oaths and for places where the Name itself is the point (ROADMAP §5.5).
- **English always stands for Israel's own language.** Sister dialects such as Moabite are accents. Everything else is spoken as itself, by your three tiers (ROADMAP §5.7).
